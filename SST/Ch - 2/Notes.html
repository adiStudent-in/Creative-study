1. Layers of the Earth

LayerDescription
CrustOutermost layer; 30–40 km thick under continents, 5–7 km under oceans
MantleThick, hot layer below crust; mostly solid
CoreInnermost; extremely hot & heavy; has Outer core (fluid, iron-nickel) and Inner core (solid, spinning, densest)
LithosphereCrust + upper mantle (rigid); broken into tectonic plates
AsthenosphereSemi-molten layer below lithosphere; allows plates to move
💡 Competency Question: If the asthenosphere suddenly became completely solid (instead of semi-molten), what would happen to plate movement and to phenomena like earthquakes and mountain building? Explain your reasoning.

2. Plate Tectonics

  • Theory given by: W.J. Morgan
  • Earth's outer layer = broken into tectonic plates (large & small) that move slowly (a few cm/year) over the asthenosphere.

Types of plates:

TypeCarries
Continental platesContinents
Oceanic platesOcean floors
Mixed platesBoth continents and oceans

Major plates: Pacific, Eurasian, African, North American, South American, Indo-Australian, Antarctic

Cause of movement: Convection currents in the mantle — heat from the core makes molten material rise, cooler material sinks → pushes/pulls plates.

💡 Competency Question: Using the idea of convection currents, explain why tectonic plates don't move at the same speed or in the same direction everywhere on Earth.

3. Plate Boundaries

Boundary TypeWhat HappensLandform/ResultExample
ConvergentPlates move towards each otherContinental-Continental → Fold mountains; Oceanic-Continental → oceanic plate sinks (subducts) → volcanoes & earthquakesHimalaya
DivergentPlates move away from each otherMagma rises, forms new crust → mid-ocean ridgesMid-Atlantic Ridge
TransformPlates slide past each other (no crust made/destroyed)Mainly earthquakesSan Andreas Fault (USA)
  • Ring of Fire: Belt around the Pacific Ocean where most earthquakes & volcanoes occur (because many plate boundaries lie here).

TypeCause

5. Erosion (Breaking Down + MOVEMENT of material)

TypeCaused By
Water erosionRivers, rain, ocean waves
Wind erosionCommon in dry, sandy areas
Glacial erosionMoving ice scrapes & carries rock
Coastal erosionSea waves wearing shoreline

Effect on human occupations:

  • Farmers → lose fertile topsoil → lower crop yield
  • River/coastal dwellers → lose land, houses, roads
  • Construction/mining → land becomes unstable, safety risk
  • Tourism & fishing → beaches/rivers/fertile land destroyed
💡 Competency Question: A village near a river depends on farming, fishing, and tourism. Explain how a single event of severe water erosion could affect all three livelihoods together.

6. Agents of Gradation

(Forces that wear down, transport & deposit material to level the Earth's surface)

  • Running water → erodes to form valleys & plains
  • Glaciers → carve U-shaped valleys
  • Wind → shapes deserts (erosion + deposition of sand)
  • Waves → erode coastlines (cliffs, beaches, bays)
  • Groundwater → dissolves rock (limestone) → caves, sinkholes
💡 Competency Question: Classify the following into agents of gradation and explain your choice: melting glacier carving a valley, sea waves forming a cliff, river depositing silt at its mouth.

7. Landforms Made by Running Water

River course pattern:

CourseLandforms Formed
Upper course (steep)V-shaped valleys, waterfalls, rapids
Middle courseMeanders, floodplains
Lower courseDeltas, levees, alluvial fans
  • Waterfall: River flows over a steep drop; forms where hard rock resists erosion while softer rock below wears away. Uses: tourism, hydroelectric power, recreation, cultural/religious value.
  • Meander: Winding bend in middle/lower course; outer bank eroded, inner bank gets deposition → large loops. Importance: fertile soil for farming, settlement sites, navigation/irrigation/tourism (e.g., Grand Anicut/Kallanai, Tamil Nadu).
  • Delta: Fan/triangular landform at river mouth where sediments deposit into sea/ocean/lake. Highly fertile (rice, jute); supports fishing, trade, dense settlement; but flood-prone.
💡 Competency Question: A river forms a waterfall in its upper course and a delta at its mouth. Explain why these two very different landforms can be produced by the same river.

8. Landforms Made by Waves and Currents

  • Beach: Sand/pebbles/rocks deposited by waves along shore. Importance: tourism, fishing, sand/shell collection, natural barrier against erosion.

Coastal erosion landforms (sequence):

Cliff (waves undercut coast) → Wave-cut platform (flat area left as cliff retreats) → Cave (waves erode weak rock) → Arch (caves from both sides of headland meet) → Stack (arch collapses, leaving isolated rock pillar)

💡 Competency Question: Arrange the following in the correct order of formation and justify the sequence: sea stack, sea cave, sea cliff, sea arch.

9. Landforms Made by Glaciers

Erosional landforms:

  • U-shaped valley – glacier widens/deepens a valley
  • Cirque – bowl-shaped depression at glacier's head
  • Arete – sharp ridge between two valleys
  • Hanging valley – where a small glacier meets a larger one
  • Fjord – sea floods a glacial valley (deep, narrow inlet)

Depositional landforms — Moraines

(deposited rock/soil/debris called till):

TypeFormed
Lateral moraineAlong the sides of a glacier
Terminal moraineAt the end of a glacier (marks furthest advance)
Medial moraineWhere two glaciers meet, lateral moraines join
  • Human importance: tourism (trekking, skiing), fertile soil, natural dams/lakes for water supply, hydroelectric power, and glaciers as fresh water source for rivers.
💡 Competency Question: If global warming causes a glacier to melt back rapidly, which glacial landform would help scientists identify exactly how far the glacier had originally advanced? Explain.

10. Landforms Made by Wind

Erosional landforms:

  • Yardang – streamlined rock ridge carved by wind
  • Ventifact – rock polished/shaped by sandblasting
  • Deflation hollow/blowout – shallow depression where loose material removed
  • Desert pavement – flat surface left after fine particles blown away

Depositional landforms — Dunes:

TypeShape/Cause
BarchanCrescent-shaped; limited sand, single wind direction
LongitudinalLong ridges parallel to prevailing wind
StarMultiple arms; winds from different directions
ParabolicU-shaped, often stabilised by vegetation
  • Human importance: barrier against desertification, tourism/adventure sports, coastal protection, construction sand.
💡 Competency Question: A desert region has wind blowing mainly from one direction with limited sand supply. Which type of dune is most likely to form there, and why?

11. Landforms Made by Underground Water (Karst Topography)

Formed by chemical weathering/erosion of soluble rocks (like limestone):

  • Caves – hollow spaces from acidic water dissolving rock
  • Stalactites – icicle-shaped, hang from cave ceiling
  • Stalagmites – rise from cave floor
  • Sinkholes/dolines – depressions from ground collapsing into a cavity
  • Underground rivers – flow through cave systems
  • Human importance: fresh water source, tourism, cultural/religious significance, geological study.
💡 Competency Question: How would you distinguish a stalactite from a stalagmite just by looking at a cave photograph? Explain the difference in their formation.

12. Landforms and Disasters

💥 Landslides

  • Heavy rainfall, earthquakes/volcanic eruptions, steep slopes with loose rock, deforestation, mining, unplanned construction, poor drainage

❄️ Avalanches

  • Heavy snowfall in short time, weak/loose snow layers, sudden temperature rise (melting reduces friction), strong winds piling snow, earthquakes/vibrations, human activity (skiing, construction)

🌊 GLOFs (Glacial Lake Outburst Floods)

  • Rapid glacier melting (rising temperature) increasing lake water/pressure, heavy rain/snowfall adding water, earthquakes/avalanches/landslides weakening the natural ice/moraine dam → sudden collapse

🌪️ Dust Storms

  • Strong winds lifting dry loose soil/sand, prolonged drought, low rainfall, sparse vegetation (deforestation/overgrazing/poor farming), climate change
💡 Competency Question: A hilly region experiences deforestation, followed by unusually heavy rainfall. Predict the disaster most likely to occur and explain the chain of causes leading to it.

13. Humans and Landforms — Connection

  • Rivers/plains (Ganga, Nile, Brahmaputra, Indus) → gave rise to agriculture & early cities
  • Mountains → barriers and connectors (Himalayas shielded India from invasion but Khyber Pass allowed cultural exchange)
  • Deserts (e.g., Thar) → limited settlements but encouraged trade routes (e.g., Silk Route)
  • Coasts/harbours → supported trade, travel, cultural contact (helped south Indian kingdoms flourish)
💡 Competency Question: "Landforms act as both barriers and bridges for human civilisation." Justify this statement with two examples from the chapter.

⚡ Quick Recap (Must Remember)

  • Earth's layers: crust, mantle, core
  • Internal forces (earthquakes, volcanoes, folding, faulting) → build up landforms
  • External forces (weathering, erosion, deposition) → wear down/reshape landforms
  • Agents of gradation: running water, waves/tides, glaciers, wind, groundwater
  • Disasters linked to landforms: landslides, avalanches, GLOFs, dust storms

📖 Key Terms Glossary

Landform
Natural feature on Earth's surface formed by weathering, erosion, deposition, and crustal movement
Weathering
Breaking down of rock without movement
Erosion
Breaking down and movement of material
Contouring
Trenches dug along hillside contours to slow/hold rainwater, prevent soil erosion, recharge groundwater
Bunding
Earthen embankments along contours to reduce run-off & soil erosion
Terracing
Level steps cut into a hillside to prevent soil erosion
Till
Rock/soil/debris carried and deposited by a glacier (forms moraines)
