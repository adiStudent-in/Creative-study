## 1. Earth as One System — Five Interacting Spheres

- **Core idea:** Life on Earth is powered by constant flow of **energy and matter**. Main source = **Sun**. Also: Earth's hot interior + chemical reactions in air, water, rocks.
- Earlier you learnt pieces separately (sunlight → winds/water cycle; plants/microbes → nutrients; tilt → seasons; humans → air/water/soil/climate). Here they are joined as **one Earth system** of interacting 'spheres':

| Sphere | What it includes | Examples from chapter |
|---|---|---|
| **Geosphere** | Solid rocks, soil, landforms, Earth's interior | Deccan plateau, Thar desert |
| **Hydrosphere** | Liquid water — surface water + groundwater | Oceans, Ganga–Brahmaputra rivers, lakes, groundwater |
| **Cryosphere** | Solid water — ice and snow | Himalayan glaciers, snow in Ladakh, polar ice caps |
| **Atmosphere** | Air surrounding Earth | Cleaner air in mountains/forests |
| **Biosphere** | All living organisms + habitats | Mangroves, forests, farms, ocean plankton, coral reefs |

- **Connection rule:** Natural processes — heating by solar radiation, movement of air and water, nutrient cycling — connect spheres in delicate balance. **Disturbance in one → changes in others.**

- Clearing large forest → affects river flow in that area.

- Rising CO₂ → affects ocean plankton.

## 2. Uneven Heating of the Earth — Solar Radiation

### 2.1 Sun's energy and EM spectrum

- Solar radiation reaches Earth as **electromagnetic (EM) waves** travelling through **vacuum at speed of light: 3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹**. Unlike EM waves, **sound waves** (Ch. 10) are mechanical and need a medium.
- EM spectrum: high frequency / short wavelength = gamma rays, X-rays (very high energy, harmful); low frequency / long wavelength = infrared, radio waves.
- **Solar radiation reaching Earth is ~99% in UV + visible + IR.**
  - Gamma/X-rays mostly **filtered by upper atmosphere**; microwaves/radio carry very little energy to warm Earth.
  - **UV (100 nm – 400 nm; 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m):** much higher energy than visible. Mostly absorbed by **ozone layer** (protects life + heats atmosphere). Overexposure damages eyes/skin, raises cancer risk → use UV glasses/sunscreen. Useful: killing germs in water purifiers, fluorescent lights.
  - **Visible light:** reaches surface; energy for **photosynthesis** (primary food source); partly warms land/water.
  - **Infrared:** warms surface; surface re-radiates heat; portion trapped by greenhouse gases — **CO₂, CH₄, water vapour** — keeping Earth warm enough for life.

### 2.2 Insolation and solar constant

- **Insolation** = amount of Sun's radiation reaching Earth's surface.
- **Solar constant** = average solar energy per unit time per unit area perpendicular to Sun's rays **at top of atmosphere ≈ 1.4 kW m⁻²** (= 1400 J s⁻¹ m⁻²), before absorption/scattering/reflection.
- Gases, clouds, dust absorb/scatter, so **maximum insolation at surface ≈ 1 kW m⁻² under clear sky**.
- India, in tropical/sub-tropical location, gets **abundant sunlight year-round** → drives southwest monsoon (climate + agriculture) + huge solar-energy potential.

**Worked Example (Example 13.1):**
How much solar energy will a 1 m² area receive in 1 hour if insolation = 1 kW m⁻²?
E = Intensity × area × time
E = 1000 J s⁻¹ m⁻² × 1 m² × 3600 s = **3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J**
= energy to melt ~5 kg ice and heat water to 100 °C = **one unit of household electricity**.

## 3. How Surfaces Respond — Albedo and Re-radiation

- **Land heats faster than water.** Variation also by material and colour. **Dark surfaces absorb more, light surfaces reflect more and stay cooler.** E.g. dark roads hotter than light surfaces; dark clothes hotter than white in summer.
- **Albedo** = fraction of solar radiation reflected by a surface (Latin *whiteness*). High albedo → cool (reflects more); low albedo → heats fast (absorbs more).

- Snow/ice high albedo → polar regions very cold. Black soil/ocean water low albedo → relatively warmer.
- **Fig. 13.5 path:** Sun → partly reflected by atmosphere/clouds; partly absorbed by atmosphere/clouds; partly absorbed by Earth's surface; partly reflected by Earth's surface.
- **All objects radiate heat.** Concrete house feels hot at night in summer due to re-radiated heat; thick mud + wooden traditional houses stay cool (less re-radiation).

**Threads of Curiosity — Urban Heat Island Effect (Fig. 13.6):**
Cities warmer than rural surroundings, especially summer/night. Built-up steel, concrete, brick, asphalt absorb + retain heat; re-radiation warms city. More AC demand stresses urban ecosystems. Rural/forest areas cool via shade + transpiration. Shows human land use alters local climate.

## 4. Latitude, Shape and Tilt — Why Equator Is Warm

- Earth is **spherical** → Sun's rays strike latitudes at different angles.
- Equatorial radiation concentrated over **smaller area**; polar radiation spread over **larger area**. Equator warm year-round; poles much colder. Creates equator-to-pole temperature difference.
- Spherical shape + **tilt of rotation axis** → **seasons** and changing day length during one revolution around Sun.
- Net result: solar radiation NOT evenly distributed → drives **global winds and ocean currents**.

## 5. Role of the Atmosphere — Composition and Layers

- Atmosphere = air held by **Earth's gravity**. Mainly **nitrogen 78% + oxygen 21%**, plus small argon, CO₂, water vapour, other gases.
- **Layered structure (Fig. 13.7, Table 13.2):**

| Layer | Approx. altitude | Features (chapter) |
|---|---|---|
| **Troposphere** | **0–12 km** (avg ~12 km) | Nearly all weather here; heated from Earth's surface; temp **decreases ~6.5 °C/km**; warm air rises → winds/storms; height max above equator, lowest above poles |
| **Stratosphere** | **12–50 km** | **Ozone layer** absorbs UV → temp **increases with height**; rise calms layer (no vertical mixing) → weather confined to troposphere |
| **Mesosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere** | Above stratosphere | Only minor role in surface climate |
| **Outer space starts** | **~100 km** | Boundary given in chapter |

- **Two protective roles:**
  1. **Partly absorbs incoming solar:** ozone blocks harmful UV; clouds/gases absorb some sunlight (Fig. 13.5).
  2. **Traps outgoing heat:** surface absorbs sunlight, re-radiates in **infrared**; greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, water vapour) absorb it, preventing escape. Without atmosphere Earth would be too cold. But **excess CO₂ enhances greenhouse effect → global warming → potentially uninhabitable**.
- **Venus vs Mercury:** Venus hotter than Mercury though Mercury is closer — because Venus atmosphere caused **uncontrolled greenhouse effect** (Grade 8 link).
- Atmosphere continuously modified by solar radiation + cosmic rays → affects weather, climate, communication. Makes Earth unique.

**Meet a Scientist — K. R. Ramanathan:** Climbed to **18,000 feet in Himalayas in 1934** to measure ozone; found lower than expected; laid foundation for UV-vs-altitude/pollution understanding; later led early monsoon forecasting.

**Why ozone matters + Montreal Protocol:** Ozone shield absorbs harmful UV. **CFCs** (refrigerators, aerosols) destroyed it faster than formed → **ozone hole over Antarctica** late 20th century → more UV harms organisms/ecosystems. Global **Montreal Protocol** cut CFCs → layer slowly recovering — power of cooperation. **Kyoto Protocol + Paris Agreement** (CO₂ cuts) less successful so far.

## 6. Uneven Heating Causes Wind and Ocean Currents

- Wind = air from **high to low pressure**, driven mainly by uneven heating. Same idea explains land/sea breezes (earlier grades) up to planetary scale.

### 6.1 Local winds — valley and mountain breezes (Fig. 13.8)

| Breeze | When | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Valley breeze (day)** | Daytime | Sun-facing slopes heat faster than valley floor → warm air over slopes rises (low pressure) → cool valley air flows **up-slope** |
| **Mountain breeze (night)** | After sunset | Slopes lose heat faster, become cooler; valley floor relatively warmer → cool dense slope air flows **down into valley** |

- Common in **Shimla, Dehradun, Himalayan valleys**. Influence temperature, moisture, soil/crop health, daily life.

### 6.2 Planetary winds — pressure belts (Fig. 13.9)

- **Equatorial low:** intense heating → warm air rises at **0°**.
- Air moves poleward aloft, cools, sinks ~**30° N/S → sub-tropical high** belts. Part returns to equator at surface (one circulation); part moves poleward, rises ~**60° N/S** meeting polar cold air → **sub-polar low** belts.
- **Polar highs ~90° N/S:** very cold dense air sinks → flows toward sub-polar belts.
- **Rotation deflects** winds to curved paths: **right in Northern Hemisphere, left in Southern Hemisphere**.

> 🧠 **Competency-Based Question:**
> Using Fig. 13.9, trace one full surface loop starting at 30° N high pressure: which way does air flow at the surface toward the equator vs toward 60° N, and how does rotation bend each path in the Northern Hemisphere?

### 6.3 Ocean currents (Fig. 13.10)

- Continuous movement of large ocean water masses. Strong planetary winds **drag surface water by friction**; plus temperature/salinity differences, Earth's rotation, land distribution.
- Warm equatorial surface water → poles; colder denser water → equator at depth. Lower salinity (less dense) stays near surface; higher salinity (denser) sinks to deep levels.
- Rotation forms large circular **gyres: clockwise in Northern Hemisphere, counter-clockwise in Southern**. Continents block/redirect.
- **Regulate climate + support life:** transport heat equator→poles, reduce contrasts. Example: **North Atlantic Drift** (extension of **Gulf Stream** carrying warm water from southern North American east coast across Atlantic) → NW Europe, keeps many high-latitude ports **ice-free in winter** → supports trade/commerce. Also transport nutrients → massive ecosystems.

**Pause and Ponder (chapter):** How does cool mountain breeze benefit crops/soil? What happens to warm equatorial surface water moving poleward and its impact?

## 7. Biogeochemical Cycles — Matter + Energy Recycled

- Living + non-living (abiotic-biotic) exchange matter/energy continuously → **cyclic movement = biogeochemical cycle**. Recycles C, N, O; maintains balance; helps ecosystems recover.
- Focus: water, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen — balance matters, interlinked across spheres, sensitive to humans.

### 7.1 Water cycle (Fig. 13.12)

- Terms (Grade 7): **evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, groundwater**.
- Path: evaporate from rivers/lakes/oceans → condense to clouds → precipitate (rain/hail/snow) → run off back to ocean; some seeps as groundwater. Dissolves minerals from soil/rocks, supports terrestrial life, carries nutrients to oceans → marine life.
- **Climate-change disruption:** warmer air holds more moisture → heavier rains somewhere (intensified monsoons) + droughts elsewhere. Melting glaciers → more river water, long-run sea rise → threat to **Mumbai, Chennai**. Intense bursts → more run-off (erodes soil) + less infiltration → less groundwater recharge → hard to sustain agriculture in dry months. Links cryosphere + hydrosphere + atmosphere + geosphere + biosphere.

### 7.2 Carbon cycle (Fig. 13.13, 13.14)

- Carbon = backbone of proteins, carbs, fats, DNA. Circulates: atmosphere (CO₂) ↔ biosphere (plants/animals) ↔ geosphere (carbonate rocks, coal/oil) ↔ hydrosphere (dissolved CO₂, marine shells).

| Cycle speed | Time scale | Path |
|---|---|---|
| **Fast cycle** | Days–years | Photosynthesis: plants convert CO₂ → glucose; respiration returns CO₂; animals eat plants/animals; death + decomposition return CO₂ |
| **Slow cycle** | Millions of years | Buried dead matter → coal/oil/gas; burning releases CO₂ very fast; ocean-atmosphere exchange: ocean absorbs CO₂ → carbonate/bicarbonate → phytoplankton photosynthesis + shells; dead sink → long storage |

- **Keeling curve (Fig. 13.14):** **315 ppm (1960) → 420 ppm (2025), ~35% rise**, unprecedented in human civilisation. Sawtooth = seasonal dips from Northern Hemisphere plant growth absorbing CO₂. ppm = parts per million.
- Some CO₂ needed to keep warm, but excess intensifies greenhouse → warming, glacier/Arctic melt, sea rise, extremes. In India: more intense monsoons (warmer air holds moisture), threats to agriculture via rainfall shifts. Fossil fuels still power much electricity; rapid renewables growth helps.
- **Threads:** Carbon ~**49% of dry weight** of organisms; **71% of global carbon in oceans** (regulates atmospheric CO₂); atmosphere holds only **~1%**.

### 7.3 Nitrogen cycle (Fig. 13.15)

- N essential for **proteins + nucleic acids**. Largest reservoir = atmosphere, but **N₂ non-reactive**, unusable directly → must become soluble compounds. Steps: **nitrogen fixation → assimilation → ammonification → nitrification → denitrification**.

| Step | Bacteria / process | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixation** | *Rhizobium* (legume root nodules), *Azotobacter* (soil) | N₂ → **ammonia (NH₃)** |
| **Nitrification** | *Nitrosomonas* | ammonia → **nitrite (NO₂⁻)** |
| **Nitrification** | *Nitrobacter* | nitrite → **nitrate (NO₃⁻)** |
| **Assimilation** | Plants (animals via eating) | absorb N compounds from soil |
| **Ammonification** | Decomposers (bacteria/fungi) on dead/waste | organic matter → **ammonia** to soil |
| **Denitrification** | *Pseudomonas* | nitrates → **N₂ gas** back |
| Extra natural | **Lightning** | tiny N₂ → nitrogen oxides |

- **Ready to Go Beyond — Haber-Bosch (early 1900s):** artificial N₂ → ammonia for fertilisers; 'Bread from Air' → Green Revolution, feeds billions. **>Half N atoms in human body from Haber-Bosch.** Energy-intensive (**~1–2% global energy**); fertiliser overuse degrades soil/water.

### 7.4 Oxygen cycle (Fig. 13.16)

- **~21% atmosphere = free O₂.** Component of carbs, proteins, nucleic acids, fats. Also combined: crust metal oxides/minerals, air CO₂.
- Focus = regulate atmospheric O₂: **consumption** = respiration (plants/animals) + combustion (uses O₂ → CO₂); **production** = **photosynthesis** (sunlight + water + CO₂ → glucose + O₂). Balance circulates O₂ across atmosphere/land/oceans/organisms.

## 8. Human Impact on Earth's Processes

| Action | Sphere disruption | Effect (chapter) |
|---|---|---|
| Fossil burning + deforestation | Carbon sinks (forests/oceans) saturated (past 50 yrs major shift) | Excess CO₂ → warming; more ocean absorption → **acidic seawater** → threatens **plankton + coral reefs**; but warmer water **reduces** ocean's CO₂ sink capacity |
| Excess fertiliser nitrates to rivers/lakes | N cycle overload | **Eutrophication (Fig. 13.17): algal blooms** deplete O₂, kill fish; threatens water bodies + coastal fisheries |
| Deforestation | Air/water/land/organisms | Less photosynthesis + transpiration → less local rainfall; altered **albedo**; no roots → more **soil erosion**; habitat loss → **biodiversity decline** |
| Vehicular emissions + sunlight | City air | **Ground-level smog + ground-level ozone (harmful)** — contrast stratospheric ozone (protective) |
| Rising CO₂ generally | All spheres | Extreme weather, biodiversity loss |

- **Solutions (chapter):** local + global cooperation. Montreal Protocol recovering ozone; Kyoto/Paris CO₂ cuts less successful. Conserve energy/resources, switch to **solar/wind**, plant trees (India planted billions, expanded solar/renewables), save water, sustainable farming, individual: save water/food/energy, reduce waste, reuse/recycle.
- **Threads — Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment):** India-led global initiative at **UN Climate Change Conference 2021**. Mindful eco-friendly lifestyles; traditional/ancient texts already saw Earth as interconnected via energy flow + matter cycling; simple habits (save energy, conserve) → sustainable future.

## 9. At-a-Glance (Chapter Summary Points)

- EM radiation from Sun = primary energy source.
- Most weather (evaporation, condensation, precipitation) in **troposphere**.
- Shape, latitude, axis tilt → insolation variation → uneven heating.
- Uneven heating → winds + ocean currents.
- Matter/energy cycled between biotic ↔ abiotic.
- Atmospheric O₂ used in combustion/respiration/oxide formation, restored mainly by **photosynthesis**.
- Water, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen cycle among atmosphere/oceans/land/organisms.
- Cycles make nutrients available, sustain life, regulate climate, balance ecosystems.

### Important Notes to Remember
- Spheres are **interconnected** — use Arabian Sea→monsoon, snow→lake→grass, melt→sea→cities chains.
- Insolation ≠ solar constant: **1.4 at top → ~1.0 at surface** due to absorption/scattering/reflection.
- **High albedo = cool; low albedo = warm.** Snow/ice vs black soil/ocean.
- Weather lives in **troposphere** because it is heated from below (temp falls with height); stratosphere calm because temp rises with height.
- **Venus > Mercury** proves atmosphere can outweigh distance via greenhouse.
- Winds/ocean both bend by rotation — memorise **right-N / left-S** and **clockwise-N gyres**.
- Fast carbon (days–years) vs slow carbon (millions) — burning short-circuits slow store.
- **Ground ozone harms, stratospheric ozone protects.** Do not confuse.
- Eutrophication = **excess nitrates → algal blooms → O₂ depletion → fish kill**.
