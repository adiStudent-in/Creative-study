## 🔬 Introduction

- Life originated in **water** (widely accepted); possibly in small water pools like **hot springs**.
- **Puga Valley, Ladakh** hot springs → conditions similar to early Earth (~3.5 billion years ago).
- Organisms in hot springs = **thermophiles** (heat-loving, unicellular bacteria).
- **Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences, Lucknow** studied these springs → calcium carbonate deposits may have helped form the first protective cell membrane.
- **Cell** = basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms.
- **Unicellular** = one cell (bacteria, yeast) | **Multicellular** = many cells (plants, animals).
- Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System (e.g., respiratory system).

## 🔭 2.1 How to Study Cells?

- **Limit of resolution of human eye** = **0.1 mm** (two points closer than this appear as one).
- Most cells are too small for the naked eye → microscopes needed.
- **Robert Hooke (1665)** → First person to observe a cell using a self-designed microscope (200–300X). Saw cork → named compartments **"cells"**.
- **Light microscope** → uses visible light + lenses (objective + eyepiece).
- **Electron microscope** → uses beam of electrons; reveals nanometre-scale details; used for fine cell structures.

### Estimating Cell Size (Activity 2.1)
$$\text{Estimated cell size} = \frac{\text{Diameter of visible field (µm)}}{\text{Number of cells along the diameter}}$$

**Unit:** 1 mm = 1000 µm

> 💡 **Competency Q:** If the field of view diameter is 4 mm and 20 cells fit across it, what is the estimated size of one cell? *(Answer: 200 µm)*

## 🧱 2.2 Structure of a Cell

### 2.2.1 Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane)
- **Universal feature** — present in ALL cells.
- Thin boundary (~**7 to 10 nm** thick); made of **lipids + proteins**.
- **Selectively permeable** → allows some substances in/out, blocks others.
- Described by the **Fluid-Mosaic Model**:
  - Lipid **bilayer** (heads face outward = water-attracting; tails face inward = water-repelling).
  - Proteins embedded like tiles in a mosaic → act as **gatekeepers**.
  - Molecules can move sideways → hence **"fluid"**.

### Osmosis & Diffusion
- **Diffusion** = movement of particles from high → low concentration (no membrane needed).
- **Osmosis** = diffusion of **water** across a selectively permeable membrane.
  - Water moves from **dilute (more water, less solute)** → **concentrated (less water, more solute)** solution.
- In plants → water enters root cells by osmosis.

| Solution Type | Condition | Effect on Cell |
|---|---|---|
| **Isotonic** | External solute = Internal solute | No change |
| **Hypotonic** | External solute < Internal solute | Cell swells |
| **Hypertonic** | External solute > Internal solute | Cell shrinks |

> 💡 **Competency Q:** A potato piece placed in plain water swells, while in salt solution it shrinks. Which process explains this? What would happen if a cell membrane were NOT selectively permeable?

---

### 2.2.2 Cell Wall
- Present in **plants, fungi, and bacteria** — NOT in animal cells.
- Located **outside** the cell membrane.
- **Rigid but permeable** → water and dissolved minerals can pass through.
- Plant cell wall → primarily made of **cellulose** (a carbohydrate made of many glucose units).
- **Functions:** Structural support, maintains shape, helps plants stay upright, withstands environmental stress.
- When plant cells lose water (osmosis in hypertonic solution) → cell membrane pulls away from cell wall → called **plasmolysis** — but cell shape is maintained by rigid cell wall.
- **Animal cells** (no cell wall) → shrink completely in hypertonic solution.
- Cell wall flexibility → animal cells can change shape easily → supports tissue movement.
- Cellulose in diet = **roughage** → helps in digestion.

> 💡 **Competency Q:** Why do plant cells not collapse when placed in a concentrated solution, while animal cells do?

## ⚙️ 2.3 The Cell Interior — A Coordinated Working System

### Three Basic Parts of a Cell
1. **Plasma membrane** (selectively permeable)
2. **Cytoplasm** (semi-fluid, jelly-like)
3. **Nucleus** (prominent)

### Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Primitive (nucleoid) | Well-defined, membrane-bound |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Typical diameter | 1–10 µm | 10–100 µm |
| Type of organism | Usually unicellular | Unicellular or multicellular |
| Examples | Bacteria | Plants, animals, fungi |

- **Prokaryotic:** most cellular activities occur directly in cytoplasm.
- **Eukaryotic:** has **cytoskeleton** (network of fine fibres) → structural support, maintains shape, enables movement.
- **Cell inclusions** (non-living materials in cytoplasm): starch in plant cells, calcium oxalate or silica crystals in some plant cells.

> 💡 **Competency Q:** A cell has no membrane-bound nucleus but has DNA. Is it prokaryotic or eukaryotic? Name an example.

### 2.3.1 Cell Organelles (Eukaryotic)

#### 🔵 Nucleus — "House of Coded Instructions"
- Double-layered **nuclear membrane** with **nuclear pores** (allow material transfer between nucleus and cytoplasm).
- **Nucleolus** → dense round body inside nucleus → site of **ribosomal subunit synthesis**.
- Contains **chromosomes** (visible as rod-shaped structures only when cell divides).
- Chromosomes = **DNA + specific proteins** → carry genetic information.
- Functional unit of DNA = **gene**.
- In non-dividing cell → DNA exists as **chromatin** (thread-like entangled mass).
- In dividing cell → chromatin condenses into **chromosomes**.
- **Prokaryotes** → DNA present as a single circular molecule in region called **nucleoid**.

> 🧠 *Mature RBCs in humans have NO nucleus → more space for haemoglobin → transport more O₂ → lifespan ~120 days (cannot repair/divide).*

---

#### 🟤 Ribosomes — "Protein Factories"
- Tiny structures found **freely in cytoplasm** OR **attached to Endoplasmic Reticulum**.
- Site of **protein synthesis**.

---

#### 🟠 Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) — "Manufacturing Factory"
- Large network spreading through cytoplasm; continuous with outer nuclear membrane.
- Two types:

| Type | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **RER** (Rough ER) | Has ribosomes on surface → looks rough | Protein synthesis and secretion (e.g., pancreatic cells) |
| **SER** (Smooth ER) | No ribosomes → looks smooth | Synthesis and storage of **fats and hormones** |

---

#### 🟡 Golgi Apparatus — "Packaging and Shipping Centre"
- Stacks of flattened sac-like structures.
- Functionally linked to ER, cell membrane, and other organelles.
- **Functions:** Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins/lipids into **vesicles** for transport, secretion, or lysosome formation.

---

#### 🔴 Lysosomes — "Clean-up System"
- Single membrane-bound sacs filled with **digestive enzymes**.
- Break down: unwanted proteins, carbohydrates, fats, damaged organelles.
- Products released into cytoplasm for **reuse**.
- **Sperm cells** contain lysosomal enzymes → help break outer layer of egg during fertilisation.

---

#### 🟢 Mitochondria — "Powerhouse of the Cell"
- Surrounded by **two membranes**:
  - Outer membrane: smooth and porous.
  - Inner membrane: folded into finger-like projections = **cristae** → increase surface area for reactions.
- Site of **cellular respiration** → glucose broken down → energy released.
- Energy stored as **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)** → energy currency of the cell.
- Contain own **DNA and ribosomes** → can make some proteins → share evolutionary history with bacteria.

---

#### 🌿 Plastids — "Centre for Food Synthesis" *(Plant cells only)*

| Plastid | Pigment | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Chloroplast** | Chlorophyll (green) | Photosynthesis |
| **Chromoplast** | Yellow/orange/red pigments | Colour in flowers & fruits (attracts pollinators, seed dispersers) |
| **Leucoplast** | None (colourless) | Food storage (starch, oils, proteins) e.g., potato, taro |

- **Chloroplast structure:** double membrane; has **stroma** (semi-fluid) + disc-shaped structures containing chlorophyll; starch granules stored in stroma.
- Plastids also have own DNA and ribosomes (like mitochondria).

---

#### 💧 Vacuoles — "Storage and Support"
- **Plant cells:** One large central vacuole; surrounded by selectively permeable membrane; filled with **cell sap** (water, minerals, sugars, waste).
  - Maintains turgor pressure → keeps plant firm.
  - When water is lost → plant wilts.
- **Animal cells:** Smaller vacuoles; help in temporary storage.

> 💡 **Competency Q:** Why does a plant wilt when not watered? Which organelle is directly responsible? What would happen if a plant cell's vacuole suddenly emptied?

## 🔁 2.4 How do Cells Grow and Divide?

- Cells grow up to a **certain size**, then divide → growth = increase in cell number.
- **Cell division** → new cells from pre-existing cells.
- Eukaryotic cells divide in an orderly manner by the **cell cycle**.
- ~**Hundreds of billions of cells** replaced daily in human body (~1% of total cells).

### Two Types of Cell Division

#### Mitosis
- Most common type.
- Produces **2 genetically identical daughter cells** with same number of chromosomes as parent.
- Purpose: **growth, repair, maintenance, asexual reproduction**.

#### Meiosis
- Occurs only in **reproductive organs** (testes, ovaries in animals; anthers, ovaries in plants).
- Parent cell divides **twice** → produces **4 daughter cells**, each with **half the chromosomes** of parent cell.
- Purpose: **sexual reproduction, genetic diversity**.
- During fertilisation → two gametes fuse → original chromosome number restored.

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Daughter cells | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosome number | Same as parent | Half of parent |
| Purpose | Growth, repair | Reproduction, diversity |
| Location | All body cells | Reproductive organs only |

### Errors in Division
- **Errors in mitosis** → uncontrolled division → tumours, abnormal chromosome number.
- **Errors in meiosis** → genetic disorders, developmental problems, early pregnancy loss, reduced fertility.

> 💡 **Competency Q:** If skin cells divided by meiosis instead of mitosis, what would happen to a wound? Why can't mature RBCs repair themselves?

## 📜 2.5 Cell Theory — The Unifying Principle of Biology

### Classical Cell Theory (3 points):
1. All living organisms are made up of **one or more cells**.
2. The cell is the **basic unit of structure and function** in living beings.
3. All cells arise from **pre-existing cells**.

### Scientists who contributed:
| Scientist | Year | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Matthias Schleiden** | 1838 | All plants are made of cells |
| **Theodor Schwann** | 1839 | All animals are made of cells |
| **Rudolf Virchow** | 1855 | New cells form only from pre-existing cells |

### Do Cells Live Forever?
- No. Every cell has a **definite lifespan**.
- **Contact inhibition** → in animal cells, division stops when cells touch neighbouring cells → prevents tumours.
- **Cancer cells** lose contact inhibition → divide uncontrollably → form **tumours** (benign or malignant).
- **Plant cells** do not show contact inhibition (due to rigid cell walls) → follow different growth pattern.
- **Programmed Cell Death (PCD)** → genetically regulated, organised cell destruction → essential for development (e.g., forming fingers in embryo by eliminating cells between digits).

## 🧑‍🔬 Meet a Scientist (Important for Exams!)

> ### Camillo Golgi
> - Italian scientist.
> - **1898** → First observed the **Golgi apparatus** in nerve cells of a **barn owl** using special staining techniques.
> - Early microscopes couldn't resolve it clearly → many scientists doubted its existence.
> - Later confirmed by **electron microscope** observations.
> - Structure named **"Golgi apparatus"** in his honour.

---

> ### Arun Kumar Sharma
> - Famous **Indian scientist** (botanist).
> - Known for work on **chromosomes** in plants.
> - Worked in plant **taxonomy, evolution, and development**.
> - Invented lab methods to study plant chromosomes.
> - Awards: **Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award** and **Padma Bhushan**.

---

> ### Gottlieb Haberlandt *(Ready to Go Beyond)*
> - Austrian botanist, **1902**.
> - Proposed that any living plant cell, even a fully mature cell from permanent tissue, can develop into a complete plant given suitable nutrients and conditions.
> - This ability of plant cells = **totipotency**.
> - His idea laid the foundation for **Plant Tissue Culture Technology**.

## ✅ Most Important Competency-Based Questions (Chapter-End Level)

1. Cell X is placed in pure water → swells. Cell Y is placed in concentrated salt solution → shrinks. Which option correctly explains this? *(Answer: Water moved into Cell X and moved out of Cell Y through the cell membrane — osmosis)*

2. Which pair of organelles contains DNA? *(Answer: Mitochondria and Nucleus)*

3. SER is incorrectly matched with "lipid and cellulose synthesis." Why is this wrong? *(SER synthesises lipids and hormones — NOT cellulose; cellulose is made by the cell wall machinery)*

4. What would happen if all mitochondria were removed from a eukaryotic cell? *(No ATP production → no energy for cellular activities → cell would die)*

5. Renu says plastids are in all plant parts including roots. Rohit says roots have no plastids as they don't photosynthesise. Who is correct? *(Rohit is partially correct — roots lack chloroplasts, but they do have leucoplasts for starch storage. Renu's blanket statement is not accurate.)*

6. What would happen if gametes were formed by mitosis instead of meiosis? *(Each gamete would have the full chromosome number; on fertilisation, the offspring would have double → leading to abnormality in every generation)*
