## 📖 2. *Follow That Dream* — Key Ideas (Point-wise)

1. **Opening:** "By all means follow that dream."
2. **Greatness formula:** a dream + pursuing it till it comes true = greatness. Difference between great and ordinary = **effort and sacrifice**.
3. **10-Year rule:** world-class standard in any field needs **singular, intensive pursuit for at least ten years**.
4. **The process:** **passion** for an interest → **conviction** that it is imperative to realise it → **count the cost** (years of effort, financial investment, sacrifice) → if it's still "**burning in your blood**", **plunge** — in any field: sports, science, arts, business, or design.
5. **The road:** uphill most of the way; you are **buoyed up** by knowing you're doing what you love and doing the right thing; **the prospect of success** keeps you on track when stamina runs out.
6. **Support network:** Academy Award winners thank a **host of people** — behind one winner stands a group who stayed by him/her.
7. **Why dreams stay dreams:** **wishful thinking** only (everyone wistfully says "I wish I could…"); trading the dream **for security**; circumstances changing lives (Raffles College students — the Japanese invasion in World War II changed their destiny; those who worked instead of finishing secondary school so they could **support their siblings**).
8. **Balance:** not "a wet blanket", but you "will also have to consider the **years** you need to chase your dream." After obstacles are considered, **if the burning conviction is still coursing through your veins — go ahead and act**.
9. **Dreams evolve:** life itself may change a person's dreams; new hopes "are **no less than** the original dream of younger days"; you negotiate "a **maze of hurdles**"; it takes much longer; more people join your **dreamscape**. Her example: publishing this **book** is the dream she chased for the **last ten years** — her "original dream has changed over the years."
10. **Close:** "I wish at least one of your dreams comes true too. Love, Mum."

## 📝 4. Grammar Corner

### The first conditional with an imperative
`If + present simple, (imperative).` → "if the burning conviction is still coursing through your veins, **then go ahead and do something about it**."

**Sample answers (imperatives):**
1. If the idea excites you, **go for it without delay.** 2. If you hear strange noises, **call for help immediately.** 3. If this seems too hard, **break it into small steps.** 4. If you care about the issue, **speak up and take action.** 5. If you finish early, **revise your work carefully.**

### Modal 'could' — functions
| Sentence | Use of 'could' |
|---|---|
| It **could** be in any field… (sports, science…) | **Possibility** |
| "I wish I **could** be this or the other" | **Unreal / hypothetical** |
| They **could have** preferred to trade their dream for security | **Past possibility / speculation** |
| so that they **could** support their siblings | **Past ability / purpose** |

**'could' sentences (answers):**
1. I wish **I could travel** without worrying about money. 2. They **could have taken** the shorter route. 3. I **could swim for miles** without stopping. 4. She **could be at** the library right now. 5. **Could you** repeat the question? (formal/polite request)

### Compound words with '-scape' (a view or scene of)
mindscape (view of the mind) · seascape (view of the sea) · landscape (view of the countryside) · cityscape (view of a city)

**Dialogue answers:** (i) **seascape** · (ii) **cityscape** · (iii) **landscape** · (iv) **mindscape**

### Grammar forms — 'Experiential Learning' paragraph
1. **helps** · 2. **learned** · 3. **work** · 4. **apply** · 5. **conducts** · 6. **analyses** · 7. **must reflect** · 8. **helps** · 9. **is** · 10. **will incorporate**

# 🟢 PART B — BELIEVE IN YOURSELF (POEM)

## 📖 2. The Poem (full text)

> Step up to the challenge
> There is no crowd to see,
> It's just you and the future
> And where you want to be.
> Will it pull you forward
> Or push you back in fear?
> Difficult are choices
> When the future is getting near.
> There is such ease in comfort
> To maintain the status quo,
> But this isn't what we are made for
> This isn't how we grow.
> The first step is the hardest
> There is no turning back,
> You just need to believe in yourself
> For your future to be on track.

— *Robert Langley*

| Feature | Answer |
|---|---|

| **Central idea** | "The first step is the hardest — believe in yourself and your future stays on track." |

| **Mood** | Inspirational, resolute |

| **Rhyme scheme** | Simple, effective **ABCB** per stanza (2nd & 4th lines rhyme): see–be · fear–near · quo–grow · back–track |

---

## 🛠 4. Poetic Devices (with examples)

| Device | Example | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Imagery** | "There is no crowd to see…" | **Solitary journey** — stresses individual effort |
| **Imagery** | "push you back in fear?" | **Mental barrier** that prevents growth |
| **Rhetorical question** | "Will it pull you forward / Or push you back in fear?" | Needing no answer — makes the reader reflect on choice |
| **Antithesis** | "pull you forward / push you back" · "ease in comfort… how we grow" | Contrasting pairs in parallel grammar highlight the choice |
| **Metaphor** | "The first step is the hardest" | Not a physical step — difficulty of *beginning change* |
| **Symbolism** | comfort & status quo → **stagnation**; future → **unknown** needing **courage**; first step → **leap of faith** toward **self-improvement** | Concrete images for inner experience |

**Antithesis examples from the poem:** 
- "Will it pull you **forward** / Or push you **back** in fear?" (forward↔back)
- "There is such **ease in comfort**… but this isn't **how we grow**" (ease↔growth)
This parallel contrast ("like Neil Armstrong's 'small step… giant leap'") emphasises the central choice facing the reader.

## ✍️ 6. Practice the Questions (Q & A)

**Extract 1 — "Step up to the challenge / There is no crowd to see, / It's just you and the future / And where you want to be."**
1. 'There is no crowd to see' → facing challenges is a **private act of personal responsibility** — no audience, so you act for yourself.
2. 'It's just you and the future' → **your own future is entirely your responsibility** — you alone choose where you want to be.
3. "Latha will **step up** her efforts to improve her vocal performance by practising harder each day."
4. Best title → **C. A Journey of Growth**.
5. achieve: goal :: face: **challenge**.

**Whole-poem questions:**
1. **Metaphor 'The first step is the hardest':** the start of any change is the hardest moment — fear, uncertainty and the pull of comfort sit at the beginning; once taken, the goal is within reach and you don't turn back.
2. **Antithesis message:** personal growth is a **choice between comfort and growth, fear and courage** — the balanced contrast makes it plain that we "aren't made for" a comfortable status quo; only change builds us.
3. **Realistic?** Self-belief is **essential but not enough by itself** — real success also needs preparation, practice, support and perseverance; the poem is realistic that the first step is hardest and that belief takes it.
4. **Application (your example):** a student daunted by speaking in assembly — the "no crowd", the fear, the hard first step (raising the hand); believing in yourself ("future on track") turns hesitation into growth.
