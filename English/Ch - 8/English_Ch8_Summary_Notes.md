# 🟣 PART A — FOLLOW THAT DREAM (LETTER)

## 🎯 THEME

The text is built on **three main themes**:

1. **Dreams demand effort and sacrifice** — "What differentiates greatness from the ordinary is how much effort and sacrifice people invest to realise their dream."
2. **The 10-year rule of mastery** — to reach world-class standard in any field one has to "singularly and intensively" pursue the subject for **at least ten years**.
3. **Dreams can (and do) evolve** — "life itself may change a person's dreams" — new hopes are "no less than the original dream of younger days."

**Underlying message:** follow your dream *but count the cost* — passion + conviction + commitment, yet be balanced and realistic.

> ❤️ **Message:** Follow your dream with **passion, conviction and commitment** — but first *count the cost* (years of effort, money, sacrifice), and be open to life reshaping that dream.

---

## 📝 LONG ANSWER (Complete summary — exam-ready)

> *Use this as a complete answer. Every important point of the letter is here.*

**The letter comes from *My Daughter, My Friend*, a collection by Irene Chua**, in which she shares her deepest thoughts as a mother with her daughter, **Ming**. Dated **19 June 1995** and beginning **"By all means follow that dream"**, it celebrates the special bond between a mother and her teenage daughter while equipping her for life's challenges.

**1. The truth behind greatness.** Great men and women become great because they have a dream and **pursue it till it comes true**. What differentiates greatness from the ordinary is **how much effort and sacrifice** people invest to realise their dream. In fact, to reach **world-class standard in any field**, one must **singularly and intensively pursue the subject for at least ten years**.

**2. How to decide.** It starts with **a passion for a particular interest**, then comes the **conviction that it is imperative to realise it**. She advises Ming to **count the cost** — in years of effort, financial investments and sacrifice. Then, "**if it is still burning in your blood** and you are ready to commit yourself to the task, **plunge**." It could be in any field — **sports, science, arts, business, or design**.

**3. The uphill road.** The road may be **uphill most of the way**; often you are "**buoyed up** only by the knowledge that you are doing what you love best and are doing the right thing." When stamina runs out, **the prospect of success** keeps you on track.

**4. The support network.** When winners receive the **Academy Awards** for best actors, actresses and films, they always **thank a host of people** who formed their support network. So for one winner, there is **a group of people who stood by him/her**.

**5. Why dreams remain dreams.** It is good to fulfil your dreams, but **for many people, dreams remain dreams**. Everyone has **wistfully** said "I wish I could be this or the other," yet they "have never got beyond just wishful thinking." Some preferred to **trade their dream for security**; others had their lives changed by circumstance — e.g. people who dreamt of going to the then **Raffles College (now the National University of Singapore)** but the **Japanese invasion during World War II changed their destiny forever**; and people who wished to finish secondary school but had to **go out to work to support their siblings** through school.

**6. Balance — not a "wet blanket".** Ming's mother says she is **not going to put a wet blanket** on Ming's dreams, "but you will also have to consider the **years you need to chase** your dream." After all the obstacles are considered, "**if the burning conviction is still coursing through your veins, then go ahead and do something about it.**"

**7. Dreams can change — and that is fine.** From her own experience, **life itself may change a person's dreams**; these new hopes and aspirations are "**no less than the original dream of younger days**." To fulfil them you must **negotiate a path through a maze of hurdles**; the dream takes **much longer** to realise, and the **participants in your dreamscape** are many more. As an example: **publishing this book is a dream she chased for the last ten years** — not the dream of her youth, but her "original dream has changed over the years." She is "really quite excited about it." The letter closes with the mother's wish: "**I wish at least one of your dreams comes true too.** Love, Mum."

## 👥 CHARACTER SKETCH — Ming's Mother (the writer)

A **warm, wise and encouraging mother** writing to her teenage daughter Ming in *My Daughter, My Friend*. She is **supportive** ("By all means follow that dream") yet **realistic** — she asks her daughter to *count the cost* in effort, money and sacrifice before plunging. She is **persuasive**, using **critical questions and personal anecdotes** (the Academy Award winners, Raffles College students whose destiny the Japanese invasion changed, siblings who worked to support their brothers and sisters, and her own ten-year dream of publishing this book). She reveals herself as **optimistic and encouraging** — her own dream changed over the years, yet she says the new hopes are "no less than the original dream of younger days," and she signs off with the wish that at least one of Ming's dreams comes true.

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (with answers)

**1. What do you think Ming must have written to her mother about?**
Ming must have written to her mother about **a dream she was uncertain or hesitant about**, perhaps seeking her mother's **advice or blessing** — she probably wondered whether she should pursue it, which is why her mother responds with the encouraging opening, "By all means follow that dream."

**2. How can one attain an international (world-class) level of skill in any field? Mention any two ways.**
(i) One has to **singularly and intensively pursue the subject for at least ten years**. (ii) One must **count the cost** (years of effort, financial investments, sacrifice) and, if the conviction is burning, **commit and plunge** into the task.

**3. What differentiates the mere dreamers from actual achievers?**
**Effort and sacrifice.** Great people "pursue their dream till it comes true," while mere dreamers stop at **wishful thinking** — they say "I wish I could be this or the other" but **never get beyond simply wishing** (or they trade their dream for security).

**4. How does Ming's mother use critical questions and personal anecdotes to persuade Ming and convey her message effectively?**
She poses the **critical question of "counting the cost"** — years of effort, financial investment, sacrifice — making Ming think seriously before plunging. She then supports her points with **personal anecdotes**: the Academy Award winners who thank their support networks; people who lost their chance at Raffles College because of the Japanese invasion in World War II; those who left school to support their siblings; and **her own experience** of chasing the ten-year dream of publishing this book. These real examples make her advice convincing and heartfelt.

**5. How does Ming's mother balance encouragement with caution in her advice?**
She **encourages** — "By all means follow that dream" — but couples it with **caution**: count the cost, consider the years you need to chase the dream, expect an uphill road and a "maze of hurdles." She says she is "not going to put a wet blanket" on Ming's dreams, yet insists she "will also have to consider the years." In short: **dream boldly, but plan and persevere realistically.**

**6. Is her advice still relevant in contemporary society? If yes, why?**
**Yes.** Even today, achieving world-class success takes years of focused, intensive practice and sacrifice; many young people still "dream" without action; and rapidly changing lives still reshape ambitions. Her emphasis on **effort, counting the cost, support networks and adapting one's dreams** is as true now as in 1995.

**7. (For you — personal answer.)** Be honest about the time, money, sacrifices and struggles you are willing to accept for your goal — and the ones you are not.

# 🟢 PART B — BELIEVE IN YOURSELF (POEM)

## 🎭 THEME

**Believe in Yourself by Robert Langley** is a **motivational poem about taking responsibility for your own future**. It tells the reader to **step up to the challenge** even when no one is watching, to resist the easy pull of **comfort and the status quo**, and to take that hardest **first step** — because with **self-belief** the future stays on track. Growth happens only when we **leave comfort behind and embrace change**.

---

## 💡 CENTRAL IDEA

**Facing the future alone may be frightening, and the first step towards change is the hardest — but we are not made for a comfortable status quo.** We grow only by stepping up to challenges, and **believing in yourself is what keeps your future on track.**

---

## 📜 THE POEM

> Step up to the challenge
> There is no crowd to see,
> It's just you and the future
> And where you want to be.
> Will it pull you forward
> Or push you back in fear?
> Difficult are choices
> When the future is getting near.
> There is such ease in comfort
> To maintain the status quo,
> But this isn't what we are made for
> This isn't how we grow.
> The first step is the hardest
> There is no turning back,
> You just need to believe in yourself
> For your future to be on track.

*(Poem text as printed in the textbook.)*

## 🛠 POETIC DEVICES

| Device | Example / Answer |
|---|---|
| **Imagery** | "There is no crowd to see…" → **suggests a solitary journey, stressing individual effort**; "push you back in fear?" → **evokes the mental barrier that prevents growth** |
| **Rhetorical question** | "Will it pull you forward / Or push you back in fear?" — needs no answer; it makes the reader think about which way they will let the future move them |
| **Antithesis** | "Will it pull you **forward** / Or push you **back** in fear?" and "There is such **ease in comfort**… But this isn't **how we grow**" — contrasting forward/back, comfort/growth in parallel grammar to highlight the choice |
| **Metaphor** | "**The first step is the hardest**" — metaphorical because it is not a physical step; it stands for **beginning the change**, the difficulty of making that first decision |
| **Symbolism** | **Comfort and the status quo** → *stagnation* and fear of change (the comfort zone that holds one back); **the future** → the *unknown* that requires *courage*; **the first step** → the initial *leap of faith* that begins *self-improvement* / personal development |

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (with answers)

**1. What does the line 'There is no crowd to see' suggest about facing challenges?**
That facing challenges is a **private, solitary act** — there is no audience applauding you. You must act on **your own belief and responsibility**, not for show or encouragement from others.

**2. Complete: 'It's just you and the future' suggests that —**
**the responsibility for your own future rests solely on you** — you alone decide where you want to be and how to get there.

**3. Fill the blank (word from the extract) — "Latha will ____ her efforts to improve her vocal performance by practising harder each day."**
**step up** (— "Step up to the challenge").

**4. Most suitable title for the extract:** **C. A Journey of Growth.**

**5. Complete the analogy — achieve: goal :: face: ______.** **challenge.**

**6. Significance of the metaphor 'The first step is the hardest' in the context of personal growth.**
It captures the truth that **beginning is the most difficult part of any change** — the decision to step out of comfort, the fear, and the uncertainty all sit at the start. Once the first step is taken, the goal is already in reach, and **momentum and self-belief** carry you forward; "there is no turning back."

**7. What message does the antithesis convey about the nature of personal development?**
That personal development means **choosing growth over comfort, forward over fear** — the parallel contrasts ("pull you forward / push you back", "ease in comfort / how we grow") make us see that **staying in the status quo is not living up to what we are made for**; only change and challenge build us.

**8. Is the poet's message realistic in the context of real-world struggles?**
**Largely, with a caveat.** "Believing in yourself" is **essential but not always sufficient** — real obstacles also need **preparation, practice, support from others, perseverance and sometimes luck**. The poem wisely makes the first step central (it is truly the hardest), and self-belief is what drives a person to act, prepare and persist — so the message is realistic **as a starting point of growth**.

**9. Apply the poem's message to a real situation (your answer).**
Example: a student hesitating to speak in the school assembly. The audience ("no crowd to see") and fear ("push you back") loom large; the first step — raising the hand — is the hardest ("there is no turning back"); **believing in yourself** ("the future to be on track") turns fear into confidence and growth.

**10. Select the correct meaning of 'status quo':** **2. A situation to keep things the same** — the Latin phrase *status quo* means "the state in which" / the existing state of affairs, especially when things remain unchanged.
