# 🟣 PART A — THE WORLD OF LIMITLESS POSSIBILITIES

## 🎯 THEME

The text is built on **three main themes**:

1. **Disability is not a limitation** — physical limitations don't define one's potential. *"Disability is not a limitation; it's a unique strength waiting to be unleashed."*
2. **Resilience and determination** — Dr. Deepa Malik turned her tragedy into a world of limitless possibilities instead of squandering her life in remorse.
3. **Sports break stereotypes** — Paralympics challenge stereotypes and change attitudes towards disability; when people witness the strength, skill and competitive spirit of para-athletes, it breaks down preconceived notions.

**Underlying values:** the belief in 'ability beyond disability', inclusivity and accessibility, empowering women through outdoor sports, and treating everyone with dignity regardless of ability.

> ❤️ **Message:** *Every setback is an opportunity to prove your strength. Physical limitations don't define your potential.*

## 📝 LONG ANSWER (Complete summary — exam-ready)

> *Use this as a complete answer. Every important point of the chapter is here.*

**"The World of Limitless Possibilities" is an interview with Dr. Deepa Malik, a famous personality in the world of Paralympics.** She was honoured with the Arjuna Award in January 2024 and is a **Khel Ratna, Arjuna and Padma Shri Awardee**. She has left an **indelible (permanent)** mark on innumerable aspiring athletes. The interviewer greets her with a namaste, calling it a privilege to meet such a famous personality.

**The tragedy and the decision:** At the age of 29, an awful tragedy struck Dr. Malik when she was diagnosed with a **spine (spinal) tumour**. She underwent surgery, but misfortune raised its ugly head again — the doctors declared that she would be **bound to a wheelchair for the rest of her life**, as she was **paralysed waist down**. She had two choices — **squander** (waste) her life in **remorse** (regret) or **transform it to a world of limitless possibilities**. She chose the second.

**The beginng of the Paralympics journey:** Dr. Malik loved sports and had been a **swimmer** too, so she decided to **switch to para-athletics**. This is how her Paralympics journey began. Her **breakthrough moment** came in the **2016 Rio Paralympic Games**, when she secured the **silver medal in the shot-put event**. In hindsight, she feels it was a moment of personal victory and a step forward in changing perceptions.

**Her list of 'firsts':** She is a **trailblazer (pioneer)** — the **first ever Indian female para-athlete to win an Asian Games medal in athletics** and **India's first ever female Paralympics medallist across any sport**. Her list of 'firsts' is never ending, and she has been bestowed with several **coveted (strongly desired) awards**. She says these achievements are a **testimony** to her belief that physical limitations don't define one's potential. She is a **proponent** of the school of thought **'ability beyond disability'**.

**Challenges and how she overcame them:** One of the significant challenges for her was **societal perceptions** — people often **underestimated her abilities**. Overcoming this required not only **physical strength** but also **mental resilience**. She **embraced challenges as opportunities** to prove her capabilities. The support of her **family, coupled with her fortitude (strength to face adversity)**, allowed her to turn obstacles into stepping stones.

**How sports challenge stereotypes:** Sports, especially Paralympics, have the extraordinary ability to challenge stereotypes and change attitudes towards disability. When people witness the strength, skill and competitive spirit of para-athletes, it breaks down **preconceived notions (opinions formed before knowing the truth)**. Paralympics has given her a **new lease of life** and helped her **push boundaries**. It has made her realise that individuals with disabilities can be **as capable, if not more, than their able-bodied counterparts**.

**Her advocacy for disability rights and inclusivity:** Advocacy is integral to her mission. She strongly believes in working for the cause of **strengthening the emotional health of persons with challenges** and **empowering women through outdoor sports and adventure activities**. She also supports persons from **lower socio-economic strata** and provides **equipment to para sportspersons**. She believes **youth is the voice of tomorrow**, so she organises **disability sports awareness and advocacy sessions at various schools and colleges**. Her goal is to contribute to creating a society where everyone, regardless of ability, is treated with **dignity** and given a fair chance to succeed.

**Her advice:** Every setback is an opportunity to prove your strength. Surround yourself with a support system, believe in yourself, and let your journey inspire others. Remember, **disability is not a limitation; it's a unique strength waiting to be unleashed.**

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (with answers)

**1. Explain how Dr. Malik's achievements challenge societal perceptions.**
Her achievements — winning a silver medal at Rio 2016, being India's first female Paralympics medallist, and being listed among the 10 most inspirational women para-athletes globally — prove that a person bound to a wheelchair can achieve what even able-bodied people find hard. They challenge the perception that disability limits potential and support her belief of 'ability beyond disability'.

**2. What can be the long-term impact of involving youth in schools and colleges in advocacy with respect to disability?**
Youth is the voice of tomorrow. When students attend disability sports awareness and advocacy sessions, they grow up with inclusive and respectful attitudes. This can build a future society where everyone, regardless of ability, is treated with dignity and given a fair chance to succeed — and where disability stereotypes are permanently broken.

**3. Rationalise the appropriateness of the title with reference to Dr. Malik's attitude and achievements.**
The title "The World of Limitless Possibilities" suits the text perfectly. When bound to a wheelchair, Dr. Malik did not drown in remorse; she chose to transform her life. Her determination opened up limitless possibilities for her — a silver medal at Rio, Asian Games success, global recognition, and a mission of advocacy. Her attitude shows that disability does not close the world; it can open one of limitless possibilities.

**4. "Every setback is an opportunity to prove your strength." How might this be a life lesson for every individual, and not just sportspersons?**
Everyone faces setbacks — in studies, careers, or personal life. If a setback is seen as a chance to show one's courage and effort, then no failure is final. Like Dr. Malik, anyone can turn an obstacle into a stepping stone through mental resilience, family support, and determination.

**5. Examine how her recognition as one of the 10 most inspirational para-athletes globally contributes to the larger discourse on gender equality in sports.**
It proves that Indian women with disabilities can compete with the world's best. It challenges the idea that sports is a man's or an able-bodied person's world, inspires other women to pursue athletics, and pushes the conversation of gender equality in sports forward through an actual example of success.

**6. How might Dr. Malik's thought 'ability beyond disability' serve as a guideline for success for all future para-athletes?**
It guides future para-athletes to focus on what they can do, not on what they cannot. It tells them that their abilities decide their success, not their disability, and encourages them to train, compete and dream just like any athlete.

**7. Why could the speaker switch to para-athletics quite comfortably?**
Because she **loved sports and had been a swimmer too**. Her love of sports and her past sporting experience made switching to para-athletics natural for her.

**8. The speaker calls the 2016 Rio Paralympic Games a 'breakthrough moment' because —**
it was the moment she secured the silver medal in the shot-put event — a moment of personal victory and a step forward in changing perceptions.

**9. The phrase 'in hindsight' indicates the speaker is → B. reflective.**

**10. Why did the speaker's decision to transform her life resemble a caterpillar turning into a butterfly?**
A caterpillar is limited to crawling on the ground, but a butterfly is free and beautiful. Likewise, Dr. Malik, who was bound to a wheelchair, transformed her life into one of limitless possibilities, achievements and freedom — a complete and beautiful transformation.

# 🟢 PART B — NINE GOLD MEDALS (POEM)

## 🎭 THEME

**Nine Gold Medals by David Roth** tells the inspiring story of a **Special Olympics race**. Nine athletes compete in a hundred-yard dash. The smallest and youngest runner stumbles and falls. Instead of racing ahead, the **other eight runners turn back and help him**; all nine then **join hands and walk together to the finishing line**. The race ends with **nine gold medals** and a **standing ovation**. The theme is **empathy, compassion, sportsmanship and helping others** — human compassion is more precious than winning.

---

## 💡 CENTRAL IDEA

**Victory is not always about winning — sometimes it is about kindness.** In a race where one runner fell, the eight others forgot that they were competing and **helped the fallen boy up**. All nine finished the race together, hand in hand, and were given **nine gold medals**. The poem shows that **caring for others and the spirit of togetherness is a bigger victory than any medal**.

---

## 📜 STANZA-WISE EXPLANATION (correct & complete)

### Stanza 1 — The athletes and their dream

> The athletes had come from all over the country
> To run for the gold, for the silver, and bronze
> Many weeks and months of training
> All coming down to these games.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- Athletes came from all over the country to compete for the **gold, silver and bronze** medals.
- They had put in **many weeks and months of training** — all their effort came down to these games.

### Stanza 2 — The spectators and the final event

> The spectators gathered around the old field
> To cheer on all the young women and men
> The final event of the day was approaching
> Excitement was high to begin.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- Spectators gathered around the old field to cheer the young men and women athletes.
- The **final event of the day** — the race — was approaching and **excitement was high**.

### Stanza 3 — The race is about to start

> The blocks were all lined up for those who would use them
> The hundred-yard dash and the race to be run
> These were nine resolved athletes in the back of the starting line
> Poised for the sound of the gun.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- The starting blocks were lined up; the race to be run was the **hundred-yard dash**.
- These were **nine determined (resolved) athletes** at the starting line, **poised (ready)** for the sound of the gun.

### Stanza 4 — The fall

> The signal was given, the pistol exploded
> And so did the runners all charging ahead
> But the smallest among them, he stumbled and staggered
> And fell to the asphalt instead.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- The pistol exploded as the signal; the runners charged ahead.
- But the **smallest runner stumbled (stepped awkwardly) and staggered** and **fell to the asphalt (the hard road surface)**.

### Stanza 5 — The fallen boy's anguish

> He gave out a cry of frustration and anguish
> His dreams and efforts dashed in the dirt
> But as sure as I'm standing here telling this story
> The same goes for what next occurred.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- The fallen boy cried in **frustration and anguish** — his dreams and efforts seemed **dashed (destroyed) in the dirt**.
- But what happened next was surprising and wonderful.

### Stanza 6 — The runners stop to help

> The eight other runners pulled up on their heels
> The ones who had trained for so long to compete
> One by one they all turned round and went back to help him
> And brought the young boy to his feet.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- The eight other runners **pulled up on their heels (stopped running)**.
- One by one they **turned round and went back to help him**, and brought the young boy to his feet — forgetting that they were competing.

### Stanza 7 — Walking together

> Then all the nine runners joined hands and continued
> The hundred-yard dash now reduced to a walk
> And a banner above that said (Special Olympics)
> Could not have been more on the mark.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- All **nine runners joined hands and continued**, turning the hundred-yard dash into a walk.
- The banner reading **(Special Olympics)** could not have been more fitting — this gesture represented the very ethos of the Special Olympics.

### Stanza 8 — Nine gold medals

> That's how the race ended, with nine gold medals
> They came to the finish line holding hands still
> And a standing ovation and nine beaming faces
> Said more than these words ever will.

**Meaning/Explanation:**
- The race ended with **nine gold medals** — all the runners reached the finish line **still holding hands**.
- The **standing ovation** and **nine beaming faces** conveyed the joy and honour of the moment said more than words ever could.

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (with answers)

**1. What is the effect of the phrase 'pulled up on their heels'?**
It means the runners **stopped running** — they suddenly halted, even though they had trained so long to compete. It shows that their compassion overcame their ambition to win.

**2. Why do you think all the nine contestants were given gold medals?**
Because they all showed the true spirit of the Special Olympics. They did not abandon the fallen boy; they helped him up and finished the race **hand in hand**. Their act of compassion and togetherness made **every one of them a winner**, so all nine received gold medals.

**3. What does the transformation of the hundred-yard dash to a walk symbolise?**
It symbolises that **human compassion is more important than competition and winning**. When the runners turned a race into a walk to help a fallen mate, they valued **kindness and unity** over speed and victory.

**4. What is the message being conveyed by the poem?**
The poem conveys that in the Special Olympics (and in life), **helping others and showing compassion is a greater victory than winning a medal**. The spirit of sportsmanship is to uplift one another — together, everyone wins.

**5. How does the poet's tone change from the beginning to the end of the poem?**
At the beginning the tone is excited and competitive (spectators gathering, runners poised). In the middle it becomes tense and sorrowful when the boy falls. At the end it becomes **warm, joyous and admiring** as the athletes finish together and receive a standing ovation.

**6. What is the overall mood created by the poem?**
The overall mood is **inspiring, uplifting and heart-warming** — it fills the reader with admiration for the athletes' compassion and a sense of hope and humanity.

**7. Why might the young athlete have felt on being helped by the others?**
He must have felt **grateful, relieved and deeply touched**. Instead of being left alone in defeat, he was helped by his competitors and got to finish the race with them — his sorrow turned into joy.

**8. Would you consider the incident of helping as a turning point in the poem?**
Yes. The moment the eight runners turned back to help the fallen boy, a competitive race turned into an act of compassion. This single act changed the entire course of the poem — from a story of losing to a story of **nine gold medals and togetherness**.

**9. The holding of hands signifies a feeling of —**
**unity, friendship and togetherness.**

**10. The spectators giving a 'standing ovation' indicates that they were → A. amazed.**

**11. What does the last line "Said more than these words ever will" mean?**
The standing ovation and the nine beaming faces were more powerful and moving than any words could express. The athletes' act of compassion spoke louder than language.

**12. What might be the poet's purpose of writing this poem?**
To celebrate and promote **empathy, compassion and collective support** in sports (and in life), and to show that **the spirit of the Special Olympics is about inclusion and caring for one another**, not about winning.
