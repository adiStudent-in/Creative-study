## 👤 2. About Khetaram (Part A)

- **Who:** Gramin Dak Sewak (GDS), the **sole postman of Somarad Branch Post Office**.
- **Where:** near the **Indo-Pakistan border** · **120 km beyond the last railhead at Barmer** · **50 km beyond the last phone** · **10 km beyond** where the road crumbles into sand.
- **How long:** **15 years** of service; his left shoulder **slumped** from carrying the mailbag.
- **Load & distance:** load cannot exceed **28 kilos**; **20 km** for even a single delivery.
- **Speed record:** mail delivered at the border **within 24 hours** of dispatch from Jodhpur (~330 km away).
- **Record temperature:** 49.9°C — because **50°C would mean a state holiday**.
- **Its customers:** farm families, their **money orders** from relatives, the **BSF** camp, and news of births, weddings and deaths.

## 📖 3. Carrier of Words — Read & Understand (Key points)

- **The world before internet:** mail dropped in the post box → postal system → postman → our letter box.
- **GDS = the last-mile link:** the "oases of humanity" in India's extremes (Thar, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, northeast riverine communities) stay connected only because of Gramin Dak Sewaks — **more than 50% of India Post's workforce**; many deliver in areas **inaccessible by any means besides foot** (Postmaster-General, Rajasthan Western Region).
- **Look, smell, sound of his world:** sand beds and dunes; **khejri trees**; blazing sun; swirling sandstorms; a "walking sandman".
- **The "28-kilo rule" and the "5-hour rule":** GDS work **five hours a day**; they may serve **till 65** (after 60); selection requires **another means of livelihood** — all so they can have an **assured income**, and India Post can "retain a large workforce of Gramin Dak Sewaks to serve remote outposts."
- **Khetaram's motive:** a "**new lease of life**"; "Akaal or famine is a way of life here. In a good year, I get one crop of bajra. That cannot feed my family of five. We would starve without this job."
- **Legend of trust:**
  - money orders — families rely on sums "**remitted by a relative**";
  - **Mulk Raj Anand**, *Story of the Indian Post Office*: absolute confidence Indians place in the post office;
  - attempt to **scrap the GDS** and shift the job to **patwaris** was **summarily rejected** — "We knew they would not be able to do the job" (Budh Singh, village elder).
- **A warmly received man:** dwells on any **threshold**; reads letters aloud and **drafts replies** in his slightly shaky hand; **BSF** gives him a lift (and tea when he distributes their dak); villagers offer a piece of **jaggery** for news of a **birth or weddings**.
- **The letter he dreads:** an envelope with the **right corner torn off** = news of death. He **stands outside**, reads the letter twice, then **tears it to bits** on the doorstep and mutters, "Ashubh Samachar cannot be carried into the house… Bad news must be destroyed."
- **His future:** "spine is strong"; waiting for phone lines "now only 50 km away" to become a **Gramin Sanchar Sewak** — his duty would then **entail carrying a cell phone and the post** from home to home. "**Main tayyar hoon**" (I'm ready).
- **Final salute:** people like Khetaram "are a part and parcel of our social fabric, and are a great support!"

## 📝 5. Grammar Corner

### Present Perfect Tense
**Structure:** `has/have + past participle (V3)` → "I **have studied** in this school for six years."

| Usage | Example |
|---|---|
| finished action (past event → present result) | She **has found** her book. |
| recently completed action | I **have just eaten** a mango. |
| life experience | **Have you ever seen** a play before? |
| accomplishment | I **have finished** my project. |
| change over time | Ajay **has become** a good bowler in five years. |
| multiple actions at different times | I **have seen** this book many times. |
| past action with present impact | I **have studied** in this school for six years. |

**Fill in the blanks (answers):**
1. I **have** just **got** a collection of postage stamps from my grandfather.
2. He **has collected** these stamps over a period of 20 years.
3. I **have studied** most of the stamps in his collection.
4. I **have begun** my own collection of postage stamps.
5. Grandfather **has truly inspired** me.

### Active ↔ Passive (Voice)
- **Active:** GDS delivers the mail at the border. *(subject does the action)*
- **Passive:** The mail is delivered by the GDS at the border. *(subject receives the action)*

**Khetaram's advice (passive paragraph — answers):**
- To begin with, a lot of stamina **will be needed** for this job.
- Next, reading clearly **will be practised**, as you **will be expected** to read out the letters by some of the villagers.
- Words **will be written** neatly, as you **will be asked** to draft replies by some villagers.
- Full-sleeved shirts and trousers **will be worn** to protect yourself from the heat.

### Alliteration & Metaphor (exercise)
- **Alliteration** (repetition of initial consonant sounds): "**K**hetaram's **k**haki **t**urban…"; "**s**corching **s**ummer winds and **s**wirling **s**andstorms."
- **Metaphor** (direct comparison without 'like/as'): "the desert's **furies**" (angry desert forces) · turns him into a "**walking sandman**."

## 💬 6. Writing Task — Condolence Message

**What is it?** A message that communicates your feelings to someone whose relative or close person has passed away — it mentions how sorry you feel for the loss and your **prayers for strength** to the family.

**Model (Sawant Singh → cousin Bhawar Singh):**

> 16 July 20XX
> Dear Bhawar Bhai,
> I am deeply saddened to hear about the loss of Kunwar uncle. Please accept my condolences. I extend my deepest sympathy to you and your family in this hour of grief. May God give eternal peace to the departed soul and courage to the family to bear the loss.
> Yours sincerely,
> Sawant Singh
> Bhawar Singh, Village: Veer Nagar, Jodhpur, Rajasthan 342001

**Your task:** on behalf of your parents, draft a **condolence message** for your aunt on the recent passing away of her father.

# 🟢 PART B — WORDS (POEM)

## 📖 2. The Poem (full text)

> If words could satisfy the heart,
> The heart might find less care;
> But words, like summer birds, depart,
> And leave but empty air.
>
> The heart, a pilgrim upon earth,
> Finds often, when it needs,
> That words are of as little worth
> As just so many weeds.
>
> A little said, and truly said,
> Can deeper joy impart
> Than hosts of words, which reach the head,
> But never touch the heart.
>
> The voice that wins its sunny way,
> A lonely home to cheer,
> Hath oft the fewest words to say;
> But, oh! those few, how dear!
>
> If words could satisfy the chest,
> The world might hold a feast;
> But words, when summoned to the test,
> Oft satisfy the least!
>
> Like plants that make a gaudy show,
> All blossom to the root;
> But whose poor nature cannot grow,
> One particle of fruit!

— *Charles Swain*

## 🛠 4. Poetic Devices (with examples)

| Device | Example | Message |
|---|---|---|
| **Simile** | *"words, like summer birds, depart"* | Words are fleeting, like birds of summer that leave nothing behind |
| **Metaphor** | *"The heart, a pilgrim upon earth"* | The heart is a traveller on a quest, seeking fulfilment |
| **Simile** | *"words are of as little worth / As just so many weeds"* | Many words are as worthless as weeds |
| **Hyperbole** | *"If words could satisfy the chest, / The world might hold a feast"* | Exaggeration to show that words can never truly satisfy everyone |
| **Metaphor** | *"Like plants that make a gaudy show… One particle of fruit"* | Showy, empty words are like flowerless plants — all blossom, no fruit |
| **Contrast/Antithesis** | "reach the **head**, but never touch the **heart**" | Mind vs heart; quantity vs sincerity |
| **Repetition** | the word *"words"* again and again | The poem is wholly about words — drives the point home |
| **Exclamations** | "But, oh! those few, how dear!" · "Oft satisfy the least!" | Emotion — admiration, frustration, disillusionment |

### Exclamation marks in stanzas 4–6 — the answer
The correct option is **(ii) 4. frustration; 5. admiration; and 6. disillusionment.**

## ✍️ 6. Study the Class Room Questions & Practice

### The heart and the weeds (stanza 2) — questions & answers
- **Why a 'pilgrim'?** Because the heart is a traveller on a long quest who, when in need, searches for help and comfort — like a pilgrim seeking fulfilment.
- **When would a heart 'need' words?** When it is **lonely, sad, or troubled** and longs for comfort and reassurance.
- **Words are like weeds because —** they are **worthless and useless** to a heart that really needs help (as weeds are useless to a garden).
- **Two emotions the heart feels when words are of little worth:** **disappointment** and **disillusionment** (also sadness, frustration).
- **Nature of communication suggested:** words alone are often **inadequate and hollow**; true communication needs **sincerity and feeling**, not just many words.

### The feast (stanza 5) — questions & answers
- **How can words 'satisfy the chest'?** By genuinely **comforting and healing the heart** — truly responding to a person's emotional need.
- **How can words be 'summoned to the test'?** When they are **put to the test — called upon to actually bring relief or prove their worth** in a real moment of need — they are exposed.
- **What does 'the world holding a feast' imply?** An **exaggeration (hyperbole)**: if words truly satisfied hearts, everyone everywhere would celebrate in joy.
- **Words satisfy the least because —** in real moments of need, **hollow words fail to bring true comfort** — they promise much but deliver nothing.
- **'oft' does NOT mean:** **A. always** (it means *often / usually / frequently*).

### Whole-poem questions — quick answers
1. **Words vs 'empty air':** both are **intangible, hollow, without substance** — like summer birds, words depart leaving only **empty air**, sounding like something but holding nothing.
2. **Meaningful words more precious:** "A little said, and truly said, can deeper joy impart than hosts of words." A few sincere words **touch the heart**, while many empty words **only reach the head**.
3. **Contrasting ideas → Yes:** poet contrasts **truly said words** (precious) with **hosts of words** (worthless weeds), and words that **reach the head** with words that **touch the heart** — quality over quantity.
4. **Loneliness hovers:** *"words, like summer birds, depart… leave but empty air"* (emptiness) · *"The heart, a pilgrim upon earth"* (solitary quest) · *"The voice that wins its sunny way, / A lonely home to cheer"* (explicit loneliness).
5. **Superficial words → what to do:** empty words are like **gaudy plants** that "blossom to the root" yet grow "not one particle of fruit" — impressive outside, hollow inside. **Remedy:** speak **little but truthfully, from the heart**.
