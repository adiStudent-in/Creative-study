## 🎬 4. Play Summary — Act by Act

### ACT I — The secret
- Scene opens on an **Indo-Western fusion piece in its crescendo**.
- **Iqbal's room:** small rack, wooden table **stacked with books**; posters of famous flute players **Pandit Hariprasad Chaurasia** and **Shashank Subramanyam**.
- Instrument of each child: Iqbal — **flute** · Avinash — **tabla** · Peter — **keyboard** · Shruti — **violin**.
- The **Parbatpuri Daily Clarion** writes: "Melody runs in the veins of Miss Shruti Sharma!" — Shruti is **unaffected, lost in thoughts**.
- The friends discover she has **not told her parents** about the concert. Shruti explains:
  - Papa is **strict**; he will call it a **desecration of the violin** to be played to Western tunes.
  - He believes **Classical Hindustani music, with its ragas and aalaaps, is the only kind of music an artist of any worth should play**.
  - She **cares about his opinions** but wants to try fusion too.
- Iqbal: *"All ways are right ways"* · Avinash: father will **come round** · Shruti decides it is **time to bite the bullet** — she will bring it up at dinner.

### ACT II — The refusal
- At dinner Shruti is **stuck on the last bite, lost in thought**.
- She tells her father about a concert next week. **Nabin: "No is the answer."** Rule: **one performance in every six months** — "more than that, **familiarity breeds contempt**. Work on your art and everything will follow."
- He disapproves of drowning "individual style in the hubbub of an orchestra."
- Shruti admits it is an **Indo-western fusion concert**. **Silence.** Nabin: "I never thought any of my students, let alone my own child, would lose their sense of musicality to such an extent."
- Shruti pleads — he should attend **one practice** before deciding. Nabin mocks: *"Sure, there'll be a practice, for 'phoo' music when making noise starts needing practice."* He leaves.
- Leela scolds Shruti for hiding it. Shruti says all their practice may **go down the drain**, and promises not to hide again. Leela sends her to bed.

### ACT III — The reconciliation
- At Iqbal's house. Shruti thinks (aside) she will tell them she can't join after one last practice.
- **In the middle of the performance, Shruti's parents enter and stand in a corner.** They become **engrossed**; Nabin **taps his feet, evidently enjoying**; he claps **furiously** with **pride**.
- Nabin praises her: *"You did not lose sight of the notes of the raga even once and performed it to excellence."*
- Nabin offers them the **music room** — more equipment, regular practice.
- Shruti thanks them; promises to take her art seriously.
- **Nabin: "It was Leela who opened my eyes."** Leela reveals Nabin's past:
  - Nabin's family were **vocalists** of a **highly traditional school**; they **painstakingly nurtured** the musical heritage **through thick and thin**.
  - At that time the violin had **not yet been incorporated into classical Indian music**; grandfather saw his choice as **betrayal of family values**.
  - But Nabin **worked his fingers to the bone** — "and see where the violin got him."
- **Nabin: "I underestimated the power of our own music. I was afraid you would be lost to us. I realise that my fears were baseless. After all each bay, its own wind. I trust you Shruti and I will root for your group at the concert!"**
- Shruti **hugs both her parents.** **CURTAIN DOWN.**

## 🎯 6. Themes

1. **Tradition vs modernity** — classical vs Indo-Western fusion; resolved through understanding.
2. **Duty to family vs personal passion** — Shruti respects her father yet pursues her own music.
3. **Generational gap** — the young see art as experiment; the old see it as purity.
4. **Empathy / reconciliation** — Nabin recalls his own youth and grants the freedom he once needed.
5. **Individuality in art** — "each bay, its own wind": every artist has a unique path and style.

> 💡 **Message:** *Every generation has its own wind. Respect tradition, but let love open the door to new music.*

## 🎭 7. Dramatic Devices (VERY IMPORTANT for exams)

| Device | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Aside** | A theatrical technique where a character directly addresses the audience; the other characters on stage are unaware of this speech. | **SHRUTI (Aside):** "Oh, how much I enjoy performing this piece…" |
| **Stage directions** | Words in brackets telling the actor the manner of delivering lines (not uttered aloud). | Iqbal **(a little loudly)** · Avinash **(very loudly)** · Nabin **(calmly but firmly)** · **(picking up a newspaper)** · **(getting up)** |
| **Non-lexical fillers** | Sounds like 'uh'/'um' used to fill pauses while thinking. | "**Ahem ahem**…" · "I… **er**… was finding the right words" |
| **Ellipsis (…)** | Marks a short pause or silence for dramatic effect. | "Yes, actually I… er… was finding the right words to say something to you… to you papa." |
| **Colon** | In a script, placed after the speaker's name to introduce the dialogue. | Nabin: No is the answer. |
| **Repetition for emphasis** | Repeated words to heighten effect | Iqbal / Iqbal (a little loudly) / SHRUTI (very loudly) — escalating calls |

> Q: Why no inverted commas in a play? Because dialogue in a play is shown by the **speaker's name + colon**, not by quotation marks. The brackets (stage directions) do the work of a reporting verb (like "he shouted").

## 📖 8. Grammar — Reported Speech (Exclamatory sentences)

**Rule:** For exclamatory sentences, use **'exclaimed' / 'exclaimed with (feeling)'** instead of 'said'. Remove interjections, the exclamation mark, and quotation marks.

| Direct (exclamatory) | Reported |
|---|---|
| "Wow Shruti! You're a sensation!" suggested Avinash. | Avinash **exclaimed with wonder** that Shruti **was** a great sensation. |
| "What an incredible achievement! I can't believe we pulled it off!" said Iqbal. | Iqbal **exclaimed that** it was an incredible achievement and that he couldn't believe they **had pulled** it off. |
| "Bravo! Each note was executed perfectly, and the performance was flawless!" said Shruti's mother. | Shruti's mother **applauded that** each note had been executed perfectly and the performance had been flawless. |

**Exercise V:** "Oh, how much I enjoy performing this piece!" and "Wah, how soulful!" are **exclamatory** sentences.

## ✍️ 10. Writing Task — Play Script (story start + tips)

**Story start given:** Anuradha, a teenager, learns **tabla** since primary years and is an extraordinary player. Her father, a **vocalist** and music teacher, is against her learning tabla and wants her to be a vocalist. She has been invited to perform at a prestigious event and decides to participate despite his disapproval; her mother and brother root for her. Anuradha sat wondering.

**Tips for writing the script:**
1. Write a rough draft first.
2. Mention the setting and characters.
3. Use colon (:) after the speaker's name.
4. Use brackets for the manner of delivery (stage directions).
5. Include 'aside' where needed.
6. Use non-lexical fillers (Ahem-ahem!, Arrghh!).
7. Use ellipsis (…) for pauses/silence.

# 🟢 PART B — A FRIEND FOUND IN MUSIC (POEM)

## 🧭 1. At a Glance

| **Type** | Lyrical poem of praise for music |
| **Subject** | Music as ocean, rhythm, therapy, spirit-lifter and friend |
| **Key idea** | Music is a constant, dependable companion in every mood |
| **Tone** | Grateful, warm, heartfelt |

---

## 📜 2. The Poem

> Music is the ocean
> That pulls me to the shore.
> Music is the rhythm
> That moves me to the core.
> Music is the therapy
> I need when I feel blue.
> Music lifts my spirits
> To make sure I pull through.
> The times when I'm most cheerful,
> It's clear, music was there.
> Music is the needed friend
> When no one seems to care.

---

## 📖 3. Meaning / Explanation

| Lines | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ocean / pulls me to the shore | music is a vast comforting force that draws the poet to safety and peace |
| rhythm / moves me to the core | the pattern of beats deeply affects her feelings, to the depth of her being |
| therapy / when I feel blue | music heals her sadness ("feel blue" = sad) |
| lifts my spirits / pull through | music raises her mood so she survives difficult times ("pull through") |
| most cheerful / music was there | even in happy times music is present |
| needed friend / when no one seems to care | music is her trusted companion in loneliness |

---

## 🎯 4. Theme, Metaphors & Mood

| Feature | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Theme** | Music is a friend found in every moment — a source of comfort, healing and joy |
| **Central message** | Music is a dependable companion and therapy in both happy and sad times |

**The metaphors for music (and their similes):**
| Metaphor | Meaning | Rewritten as a simile |
|---|---|---|
| Music is the **ocean** | vast, attractive, comforting | Music is as vast and comforting as an ocean / Music is as pulling as the ocean |
| Music is the **rhythm** | beats that move the body & feelings | Music is as stirring as a rhythm |
| Music is the **therapy** | healer in sadness | Music is as healing as therapy |
| Music is the **needed friend** | trustworthy companion | Music is as dependable as a friend |

**Rhyme scheme:** **aabb** per stanza (*shore/core*, *blue/through*, *there/care*) — gives a smooth, musical, song-like flow.

**Why is 'music' repeated?** To build the idea that music means everything — ocean, rhythm, therapy, friend; the repetition creates a **chant-like, rhythmic effect** matching the musical theme and emphasises how all-pervading music is in the poet's life.

---

## ❓ 5. Critical Reflection (Extract answers)

**Extract:** *Music is the therapy / I need when I feel blue. / Music lifts my spirits / To make sure I pull through.*

- **(i)** 'Feel blue' indicates **sadness / feeling low**.
- **(ii)** The line showing music makes the poet happy: **"Music lifts my spirits"**.
- **(iii)** The last line signifies that music **gives her the strength to overcome difficult times / to survive** — "pull through".
- **(iv)** "Music plays a vital role in the poet's life" — **True**.
- **(v)** Central idea: → **A. Music brings comfort during sadness.**

**Model answers:**
1. **How is music able to move the poet 'to the core'?** Its rhythm reaches the deepest part of her feelings — it affects her body, mind and emotions intensely, stirring her completely.
2. **Why compare music to a 'needed friend'?** Because it is always there when she is lonely or sad ("when no one seems to care") — it comforts and supports her like a true friend.
3. **Explain the poet's attitude towards music.** Deep love and dependence — music is her ocean of comfort, therapy, spirit-lifter and friend; it is central to her life.
4. **Universal appeal?** Everyone knows happiness, sadness, loneliness and the need for comfort; the idea of music as a never-failing friend applies to people of all ages and cultures.

## 🔑 6. Vocabulary — Phrasal Verbs

**Key phrasal verb:** **pull through** = to succeed in doing something very difficult / to survive. ("To make sure I pull through.")

**Phrasal verbs with 'move' (Exercise III):**
| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| move out | (v) to leave a place where one has been living |
| moved in | (ii) to begin living in a new home or place |
| moved on | (i) to start doing or discussing something new |
| move over | (iv) to go to a new position to make room for others |
| move along | (vi) to shift position to make space |
| moved off | (iii) to start moving; to leave |

> **Phrasal verb** = verb + preposition/adverb that together carry a different meaning. Make your own phrasal verbs from **put, get, look, break** (e.g. put off, get over, look after, break down) and write sentences.

**Format:**
| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Sender's address | (your address) |
| Date | 10 June 2026 |
| Salutation | Dear Grandma and Grandpa, |
| Body | Ask about their well-being · mention the event — date, time, venue — a Sitar recital · request them to attend warmly |
| Complimentary close | Yours affectionately, |
| Name | (sender's name) |
