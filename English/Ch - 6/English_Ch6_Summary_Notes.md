# 🟣 PART A — TWIN MELODIES (A PLAY)

## 🎭 THE PLAY AT A GLANCE

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Author** | Mitra Phukan |
| **Type** | A play (drama) in **three acts** (Act I, Act II, Act III) |
| **Main character** | **Shruti Sharma** — a young aspiring violinist |
| **Other characters** | **Guru Nabin Sharma** (Shruti's father, a violinist and Principal of Sangeetika Music School) · **Leela Devi** (Shruti's mother) · the fusion group — **Avinash** (tabla), **Iqbal** (flute), **Peter** (keyboard) |
| **Conflict** | Tradition vs modernity in music — Nabin believes Classical Hindustani music is the only true music; Shruti wants to play in an **Indo-Western fusion** concert |
| **Resolution** | Nabin watches a practice, is won over, and **supports** Shruti's fusion group — offering them the music room and rooting for them at the concert |
| **Message** | Tradition and modernity can blend ("each bay, its own wind"); parents and children can understand each other through love. |

## 📝 LONG ANSWER (Complete summary — exam-ready)

> *Use this as a complete answer. Every important point of the chapter is here.*

**Act I — The rehearsal and Shruti's secret.**
The scene opens to the notes of a beautiful **Indo-Western fusion music piece in its crescendo** (a piece of music that gets louder and louder). **Four children are on stage** in Iqbal's room, which has a small rack, a wooden table stacked with books, and walls bearing posters of famous Indian flute players **Pandit Hariprasad Chaurasia** and **Shashank Subramanyam**. Iqbal plays the **flute**, Avinash the **tabla**, Peter the **keyboard**, and Shruti the **violin**. They wind up their performance, but Shruti **wears a look of distress** on her face. Iqbal reads from the **Parbatpuri Daily Clarion**: *"Melody runs in the veins of Miss Shruti Sharma, daughter of the master violinist Nabin Sharma!"* Avinash and Peter tease her about her praise, but Shruti remains **lost in her thoughts**. They realise she has still **not told her parents** about the concert. Shruti explains that her father is strict: he will think it a **desecration of the violin to be played to Western tunes**, because Papa firmly believes that **Classical Hindustani music, with its ragas and aalaaps, is the only kind of music that an artist of any worth should play**. She does care about his opinions — she just wants to try fusion too and doesn't know how to tell him in the right way. Iqbal assures her, "All ways are right ways"; Avinash adds that he will eventually come round. Shruti decides it is time to **bite the bullet (deal with a difficult situation courageously)** and promises to bring it up at dinner that night. Peter cheers, "That's the spirit!"

**Act II — The confrontation at dinner.**
At dinner, Shruti is **stuck on the last bite and lost in thoughts**. Her father jokes about the dosa and her mother, Leela, teases her. Shruti finally gathers courage and tells her father there is a concert next week. **Nabin calmly but firmly says, "No is the answer."** He repeats his rule — **"one performance in every six months; more than that, familiarity breeds contempt. Work on your art and everything will follow."** He disapproves of her drowning her individual style "in the hubbub of an orchestra." When Shruti timidly reveals it is an **Indo-western fusion concert**, there is silence, then Nabin rises: *"I never thought any of my students, let alone my own child, would lose their sense of musicality to such an extent."* Shruti pleads with him to attend just one practice before deciding, but Nabin mocks, *"Sure, there'll be a practice, for 'phoo' music when making noise starts needing practice,"* and leaves. Leela scolds Shruti for getting into this without her father's knowledge. Shruti admits she should have asked him and that all their practice may "go down the drain," and promises never to hide from her mother again. Leela sends her to bed.

**Act III — The practice that changes everything.**
At Iqbal's house, the group begins practising. Shruti thinks (aside) how much she enjoys the piece and plans to tell the others she can't join them after one last practice. **In the middle of the performance, Shruti's parents enter the room and stand in a corner.** Slowly they become **engrossed**; Nabin **taps his feet and is evidently enjoying**; when the piece ends, he claps **furiously** with an expression of **pride**. He praises Shruti: *"You did not lose sight of the notes of the raga even once and performed it to excellence."* Leela too praises the children. Nabin then offers the children the **music room** — with more equipment, they can practise regularly. Shruti and her parents step aside; the other children practise in **mime**. Shruti thanks her parents and promises to always take her art seriously. **Nabin admits it was Leela who opened his eyes.** Leela reveals the past: Nabin himself **had to go against his family's wishes to play the violin**. His family were all **vocalists** belonging to a highly traditional school; at that time the violin had **not yet been incorporated into classical Indian music**, and his grandfather **saw the choice as a betrayal of family values and tradition**. But Nabin **worked his fingers to the bone (worked very hard)** — "and see where the violin got him." Nabin confesses: *"I underestimated the power of our own music. I was afraid you would be lost to us. I realise that my fears were baseless. After all each bay, its own wind. I trust you Shruti and I will root for your group at the concert!"* Shruti **hugs both her parents** — CURTAIN DOWN.

## 👥 CHARACTER SKETCHES

### Nabin Sharma (the father)
The Principal of **Sangeetika Music School** and a master violinist. He is **strict and traditional** — he believes Classical Hindustani music "with its ragas and aalaaps is the only kind of music an artist of any worth should play" and follows the rule of **one performance in six months** ("familiarity breeds contempt"). But he is **not unreasonable**: when he actually watches the practice, he is won over. He admits he **underestimated the power of our own music**, that his fears were **baseless**, and that his own past (going against his family to play the violin) should have taught him tolerance. He ends the play **warm, proud and supportive**. His evolution shows that love for a child can triumph over rigid tradition.

### Shruti Sharma (the daughter)
A **young aspiring violinist** in a fusion group (violin). She loves her father and **cares about his opinions** — which is why she is in a **dilemma**, unable to tell him about the concert. She is honest and brave enough to **bite the bullet** and talk to him, and she is a **talented, dedicated musician** (her father says she did not lose sight of the notes of the raga even once). She promises never to hide from her mother again. Her quiet determination and respect for her parents bring the family together.

### Leela Devi (the mother)
Shruti's supportive, witty mother. She teases Shruti gently at dinner but understands the situation. It is **Leela who "opened (Nabin's) eyes"** by reminding him of his own past — how he had to go against his family to play the violin. She is the loving bridge between tradition and modernity in the family.

### Iqbal, Avinash and Peter (the fusion group)
Shruti's fellow musicians — Iqbal plays the **flute**, Avinash the **tabla**, Peter the **keyboard**. They are cheerful supporters: Iqbal reassures her ("All ways are right ways"), Avinash jokes that it's time to **bite the bullet** and that her father will "come round," and Peter urges her to "throw a party." They bring energy and friendship to the play.

## 🎯 THEMES

1. **Tradition vs modernity** — Classical Hindustani music (ragas, aalaaps) against Indo-Western fusion; the play shows they can coexist.
2. **Duty towards family vs personal passion** — Shruti wants to honour her father's views yet pursue her own musical path.
3. **Generational gap and understanding** — Nabin's fears ("you would be lost to us") are resolved through open-heartedness, not rebellion.
4. **Empathy and reconciliation** — Nabin remembers his own past (going against his family for the violin), changes his mind, and supports his daughter.
5. **The power of art** — music unites the family; "each bay, its own wind" — every artist has a unique path.

> 💡 **Message:** *Every generation has its own wind — respect tradition, but allow love and understanding to make room for new music.*

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (with answers)

**1. Justify the title 'Twin Melodies'. How does the play explore the theme of tradition and modernity?**
"Twin Melodies" suggests **two kinds of music** — the **classical (traditional) and the fusion (modern)** — that are meant to sound together. The play is about Shruti's violin in a fusion group and her father Nabin's pure classical belief. Through the family's journey — Nabin's refusal, Leela's reminder of Nabin's own past, and Nabin finally accepting the fusion — the play shows that **tradition and modernity are not enemies; they can play like twin melodies side by side**.

**2. What are the different perspectives of Peter, Iqbal, and Avinash regarding confronting authority?**
- **Iqbal:** gentle and reassuring — "All ways are right ways… he will understand." He believes honesty and love will win the father over.
- **Avinash:** practical and light-hearted — "What's the worst that can happen? He will scold you a little… he will eventually come round." He thinks the fear of confrontation is greater than the confrontation itself.
- **Peter:** encouraging but simple — "That's the spirit!", "Tell them you come for the practice from 4 to 5." He treats it casually.
Together they give Shruti the courage to speak (to bite the bullet).

**3. Analyse the character of Nabin Sharma. How does his attitude evolve throughout the play?**
He begins as a **strict traditionalist** — "No is the answer", one performance per six months, fusion is "phoo music". His attitude evolves when he actually **watches the practice and is enchanted**. With Leela's reminder that he himself had gone against his family to play the violin, he becomes humble — "I underestimated the power of our own music… my fears were baseless." He ends **open, proud and supportive**, offering the music room and rooting for the group. His growth shows that **understanding can change a mind**.

**4. How does the play use Shruti's internal conflict to explore duty towards family versus personal passion?**
Shruti loves fusion music but does not want to hurt her father, whose classical values she respects. She fulfils her duty by **telling the truth** (biting the bullet) and showing her art seriously. In the end the conflict is resolved not by choosing one side but by **bringing both together** — she keeps her passion and gains her father's blessings.

**5. Evaluate the effectiveness of the play's conclusion.**
The conclusion is effective and realistic: Nabin changes not through a lecture but through **watching his daughter's music** and **remembering his own past**. The hug, the offer of the music room, and "I will root for your group" resolve the conflict with warmth. It shows family reconciliation achieved through love and understanding.

**6. Assess how well the role of cultural diversity contributes to the storyline.**
Diversity — flute, tabla, keyboard and violin playing together (an Indo-Western fusion) — **is the heart of the conflict**. The violin (a Western instrument) meeting Indian classical tradition, and posters of Pandit Hariprasad Chaurasia and Shashank Subramanyam, show music as a blend of cultures. This makes the play's message — that different traditions can harmonise — both musical and universal.

**7. Why is Shruti in a dilemma? State her situation in the extract.**
In the extract, Shruti is **in a dilemma** (D). She wants to play fusion, but is afraid her father will see it as **desecration of the violin**; she cares about his opinions yet wants to give fusion a try and doesn't know how to tell him "in the right way".

**8. What does Shruti mean by 'the right way'?**
She means a way of telling her father that **respects his feelings and opinions** — not hiding or defying him, but explaining her desire honestly so he understands instead of feeling hurt.

**9. Why does the extract reflect a generational gap in understanding art and tradition?**
Because the father believes classical Hindustani music is the only music "an artist of any worth should play", while the daughter sees fusion as a worthy expression. The two generations view art through different lenses — for the older, purity of tradition; for the younger, joyful experimentation.

**10. How were Iqbal's words meant as an assurance to Shruti?**
Iqbal said, "All ways are right ways, Shruti. He is your father, he will understand." He assured her that **any honest way of telling her father is the right way**, because a father's love will make him understand. This comforted Shruti and gave her courage.

**11. What does "each bay, its own wind" mean?**
It is a proverb-like phrase meaning **every bay (harbour/place) has its own wind** — every person, age, and artistic journey has its **own unique path and style**. One way does not fit everyone. Nabin says this when he accepts that Shruti's musical path (fusion) is her own wind.

**12. Why is the family's revelation about Nabin's past important?**
It explains Nabin's fear: he was afraid Shruti "would be lost" to the family just as his own father feared he was lost when he chose the violin. When Nabin remembers that he once defied tradition himself, he realises his fears are baseless and he grants Shruti the same freedom he once needed.

**13. "Shruti: There is need to worry Iqbal!" — why is there "need to worry"?**
Because she knows her papa better than anyone — he is very strict, believes fusion is a desecration of the violin, and would never understand. She is afraid of losing his good opinion.

**14. In Act II, why does Nabin mock 'phoo' music?**
He is being sarcastic and dismissive of fusion — he thinks making "noise" cannot be called music and does not deserve "practice". His mocking tone reveals his contempt for fusion at that point.

**15. What does the extract "I underestimated the power of our own music. I was afraid you would be lost to us… my fears were baseless" reveal?**
It shows Nabin's **growth and humility**. He feared fusion would take his daughter away from classical music and the family; now he sees she holds the raga faithfully even in fusion. His breakthrough **bridges traditional values and modern expressions (B)** — he understands artistic freedom.

## ✍️ GRAMMAR IN THE PLAY — Reported Speech

**Rule for exclamatory sentences in reported speech:** use **'exclaimed' or 'exclaimed with (feeling)'** instead of 'said'; remove interjections, exclamation marks, and quotation marks.

**Examples from the chapter:**
1. "Wow Shruti! You're a sensation!" suggested Avinash. → **Avinash exclaimed with wonder that Shruti was a great sensation.**
2. "What an incredible achievement! I can't believe we pulled it off!" said Iqbal. → **Iqbal exclaimed that it was an incredible achievement and that he couldn't believe they had pulled it off.**
3. "Bravo! Each note was executed perfectly, and the performance was flawless!" said Shruti's mother. → **Shruti's mother applauded that each note had been executed perfectly and the performance was flawless.**

**Exercise V — identifying sentence types:** "Oh, how much I enjoy performing this piece!" and "Wah, how soulful!" are **exclamatory** sentences.

# 🟢 PART B — A FRIEND FOUND IN MUSIC (POEM)

## 🎭 THEME

**A Friend Found in Music by Bryanna T. Perkins** is a **lyrical celebration of music as a friend**. To the poet, music is the **ocean** that pulls her to the shore, the **rhythm** that moves her to the core, the **therapy** she needs when she feels blue, the power that **lifts her spirits**, and the **needed friend** when no one seems to care. The poem shows music as a constant, dependable companion — present in both happiness and sadness.

---

## 💡 CENTRAL IDEA

**Music is a friend found at every moment.** Whether the poet is cheerful or sad, music is there — it comforts, heals, lifts, and moves her. The poem personifies music as a loyal friend who never leaves, giving the message that **music is an ever-present source of comfort, healing and joy**.

---

## 📜 THE POEM

> Music is the ocean
> That pulls me to the shore.
> Music is the rhythm
> That moves me to the core.
> Music is the therapy
> I need when I feel blue.
> Music lifts my spirits
> To make sure I pull through.
> The times when I'm most cheerful,
> It's clear, music was there.
> Music is the needed friend
> When no one seems to care.

> — Bryanna T. Perkins

---

## 📖 LINE-BY-LINE MEANING / EXPLANATION

| Lines | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Music is the ocean / That pulls me to the shore.* | Music is like a vast ocean of comfort that draws the poet towards safety and peace — like being pulled to the shore. |
| *Music is the rhythm / That moves me to the core.* | Music's rhythm (pattern of beats) deeply stirs her feelings, right to the depths of her being. |
| *Music is the therapy / I need when I feel blue.* | When she is sad ("feel blue"), music is the medicine/healing (therapy) she needs. |
| *Music lifts my spirits / To make sure I pull through.* | Music raises her mood and gives her strength to survive difficult times ("pull through" = succeed in doing something very difficult). |
| *The times when I'm most cheerful, / It's clear, music was there.* | Even in her happiest moments, music is clearly present with her. |
| *Music is the needed friend / When no one seems to care.* | When she has no one else, music becomes the trusted friend she needs. |

---

## 🛠 THE POEM'S POETIC DEVICES

| Device | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Metaphor** | A direct comparison (X is Y) without 'like'/'as' | "Music is the **ocean**" · "Music is the **rhythm**" · "Music is the **therapy**" · "Music is the **needed friend**" |
| **Personification** | Giving human qualities to non-human things | Music is called a **"needed friend"** — treated like a person who "seems to care" |
| **Simile** (when rewriting the metaphors) | A comparison using 'like'/'as' | Music is **as pulling as the ocean**; Music is **as healing as therapy** |
| **Rhyme scheme** | The pattern of end-rhymes | The poem follows the scheme **aabb** in each stanza — e.g. *shore / core*, *blue / through*, *there / care* |
| **Repetition** | Repeating words for emphasis and rhythm | "Music is…" repeated at the start of several lines — it builds up the idea that music is everything to the poet |

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (with answers)

**1. How is music able to move the poet 'to the core'?**
The rhythm of music reaches into the deepest part of her being ("the core" — the centre of her feelings). It affects her emotions so strongly that she is stirred inside, body and mind — moved like the gentle pull of the ocean.

**2. Why does the poet compare music to a 'needed friend'?**
Because music is always available when she feels lonely or unhappy ("when no one seems to care"). It comforts, understands and supports her without judging — everything a true, needed friend does.

**3. Explain the poet's attitude towards music.**
The poet **loves and relies on music deeply**. She sees it as a **constant companion** — an ocean of comfort, a healing therapy, a spirit-lifter and a friend. Music is central to her emotional life.

**4. Support the opinion that this poem has a universal appeal.**
Everyone experiences happiness, sadness, loneliness and the need for comfort. The poem's idea of music as a never-failing friend and therapy applies to people of all ages and cultures — so anyone who loves music can relate to it.

**5. Give evidence from the poem to support that the speaker is the poet herself.**
The poem is written in **first person** — "pulls **me**", "moves **me**", "**I** need", "**my** spirits", "**I** pull through", "**I'm** most cheerful". These personal pronouns and her own feelings show the speaker is the poet herself sharing her own experience.

**6. What does the phrase 'feel blue' indicate?**
It indicates the emotion of **sadness / feeling low or depressed**.

**7. Which line shows music makes the poet happy?**
*"Music lifts my spirits"* — (also "The times when I'm most cheerful, it's clear, music was there").

**8. Music plays a vital role in the poet's life — True or False?**
**True.** Music is her therapy, spirit-lifter, and needed friend who is there in all her moods.

**9. Select the central idea of the extract (A):**
→ **A. Music brings comfort during sadness.**

**10. What is the message the poet wishes to convey through the poem?**
That music is a **dependable friend and healing power** — always there in both happy and sad times, lifting our spirits and helping us pull through difficult moments.

**11. Identify the mood of the poem:**
→ **(ii) thoughtful** — the mood is reflective and heartfelt (a celebration, not nostalgic/amused/playful).

**12. What is the effect of the rhyme scheme of the poem?**
The **aabb** rhyme scheme in each stanza gives the poem a **gentle, musical, song-like rhythm**, matching the theme of music — easy, smooth and soothing to read.
