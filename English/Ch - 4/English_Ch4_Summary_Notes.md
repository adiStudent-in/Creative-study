# 🟣 PART A — VITAMIN-M

## 🎯 THEME

1. **Caring for the elderly with respect** — old age does not mean loss of memory, dignity or independence.
2. **Do not judge by age** — Grandpa seems frail and forgetful, but his memory and wit are razor sharp.
3. **Family love and humour** — the story uses gentle irony to show that it is the adults, not Grandpa, who forget things (even Papa's own birthday!).

> ❤️ **Message:** *Respect the elderly — they may need help, but never treat them as less than what they are: wise, sharp and full of love.*

---

## 📝 LONG ANSWER (Complete summary)

**"Vitamin-M" by Asha Nehemiah is the story of Grandpa, a sharp and independent old man, and what happens when his family tries to "look after" him.**

One day Ravi's mother Vidya wishes someone would invent a medicine called **"Vitamin-M"** to improve old people's memories. Grandpa is sitting in a rocking chair, frail, holding the newspaper inches from his nose. Vidya doubts he can hear, see or remember well, and is glad Ravi's holidays have started so he can take care of Grandpa. Last month had been difficult — Grandpa had come to live with them because he was too old to live alone; he had taken a **double dose of medicines** and been hospitalised, and once he **got lost** and forgot the way back.

Grandpa, however, was fiercely independent. He had looked after himself for most of his **seventy-five years**, cooking, shopping and keeping house after his wife's death ten years ago. Now, living in a small, crowded, **"poky"** city flat, he hated being forbidden to go out. He missed his **small brick house in town** with the big mango tree, where it was so quiet you could hear a leaf fall at dusk. But he had slipped and fallen in that garden one evening and lain outside all night, so his daughter locked the house and brought him to live with her.

Vidya speaks to Grandpa in an **over-loud tone**, like to a child, which makes Ravi **wince**. She leaves Ravi in charge and forbids Grandpa from going out alone. Grandpa and Ravi plan to play chess or watch cricket. Grandpa recalls **Karpov's opening move** and tells Ravi he is repeating "the mistake **Bobby Fischer** made against **Spassky**," which makes Ravi wonder how Grandpa can remember thousands of chess games but forget people's names.

Grandpa then decides to walk to the corner shop for the **Tamil newspaper**. Ravi offers to fetch it, but Grandpa, suspicious, says he's not a prisoner. He takes his **mahogany walking stick** with the brass eagle-head handle, puts on his **bright-yellow cap**, and says he'll be back before Ravi sets up the chessboard. Ravi is in a **dilemma** — if he stops Grandpa, Grandpa is hurt; if he lets him, his mother will be furious. So Ravi **follows Grandpa secretly at a safe distance**.

The "day out" is comic: Grandpa buys peanuts in a children's park, sits and watches children; Ravi, hiding behind an elephant-shaped bush, is mistaken for a fool by a little boy and shouted at by his mother, and must crawl out on all fours. Grandpa goes to a tea stall, drinks a **sugary tea**, eats **two bananas** and an **ice cream** — all banned at home. Ravi hides behind a tree and a car. Grandpa crosses the road and enters a **barber shop**. Ravi steps into the shop beside it — which turns out to be a **Ladies' Hairdressing Salon** — and is thrown out. Grandpa boards a bus. Ravi jumps on, but the man wearing the yellow cap is a **total stranger**: Grandpa had given away his cap to him in the barber shop. That is "generous Grandpa".

Ravi is frantic, rushes home — and is overjoyed to find Grandpa **snoring, safe in his bedroom**. He hugs him. When Vidya comes home, Grandpa coolly says he had "a quiet morning," hinting that Ravi had disappeared. Then the twist: Grandpa brings out **gift-wrapped parcels** for everyone. Vidya says, "But Papa, Ravi's birthday was three months ago! Have you forgotten?" Grandpa: "It's my birthday today." Vidya had circled *Papa's birthday* in red on the calendar at the beginning of the year — and forgot it. Grandpa gives Ravi a hardcover book, *The Best Detective Stories*, "… how to avoid getting fooled when trailing a suspect," saying it with a twinkle in his eye. It is clear Grandpa had **known** all along that Ravi was following him.

**The central irony:** It is **not Grandpa** who needs a memory pill ("Vitamin-M") — it is the adults, especially **Vidya**, who forgot the birthday.

## 👥 CHARACTER SKETCHES

### Grandpa
A **retired lawyer, seventy-five**, who misses his quiet brick house with the mango tree. To his family he seems frail and forgetful (they whisper about his memory), but he is actually **sharp-witted and playful**: he remembers Karpov and Fischer, teases and deceives Ravi's surveillance with a swapped cap, and surprises everyone with gifts on *his own* birthday. He is **loving, generous, independent, dignified and witty** — never a child, always a master.

### Ravi
A **thoughtful, caring boy** given to his grandfather's care. He respects his mother yet **dislikes treating Grandpa like a child**. He follows Grandpa secretly like a **good detective**, endures embarrassment and danger, but is finally **filled with relief and affection**. His love for Grandpa shows in the hug at the end.

### Vidya (Mamma)
Ravi's mother and Grandpa's daughter. She is **loving and responsible** but also **over-protective and a little condescending** — she speaks to her father in a loud, childlike tone and forgets his birthday. The story's message — *the elderly deserve respect and not pity* — is addressed at her over-care.

### Ravi's father
A quiet, supportive man, usually in the background, part of the family that Grandpa provokes with his "babies" and gifts.

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (Competency-based) — with answers

1. **Why was this story 'Vitamin-M'?** The mother wishes for a memory medicine ("Vitamin-M") to give old people. The irony is that **Grandpa's memory is fine** — it is Vidya who forgets Papa's birthday. "Vitamin-M" is a symbol for the **reminder, respect and care** that our elders deserve.

2. **Why did Grandpa dislike the city?** He **hated the noise and bustle** of the city, and **longed for the quiet of his brick house in town**, where he could even hear a leaf fall. It was the city that imprisoned him.

3. **Why did Vidya not let Grandpa go out alone?** Because he had **taken a double dose of medicine** (hospitalised) and **got lost once**, forgot the way back. She was over-protective.

4. **When Grandpa was going out, why did he say "Told you to keep me a prisoner here, has she?"** Because Grandpa, sharp-minded, **guessed the mother's instruction** to Ravi; he resented being treated like a prisoner.

5. **Was it easy for Ravi to follow Grandpa?** No — he was spotted by a little boy and shouted at, caught at the park, scolded at tea-stall, thrown out of the Ladies' salon, and jumped on the wrong bus → he was an **amateur detective**.

6. **Was Grandpa aware that Ravi was following him?** Yes — the knowing **twinkle** in his eye and the gift of the detective book ("How to avoid getting fooled when trailing a suspect") prove that Grandpa had understood everything.

7. **Who needs a possibly mental 'Vitamin-M'?** In the end — **Vidya**. She forgot her own father's birthday, though she had marked it on the calendar. The title is a gentle joke: it is adults who need to be mindful, not Grandpa.

8. **What does the gift of the detective book show?** Grandpa wants to have a frank and playful reminder with Ravi — it shows that **Grandpa had observed Ravi's trailing** and was **wise, not failing**, and that he uses a humorous, loving way rather than an angry one.

9. **"I had a quiet morning, but I don't know about Ravi" — explain.** Grandpa coolly tells his mother that he had a quiet morning while Ravi just "disappeared." This is his cunning, teasing way of letting Ravi know that he had been fully aware of being followed all day — delivered without anger, only humour.

10. **What's the irony in the tale?** The adults think Grandpa is old and forgetful and make a "Vitamin-M" joke — but at the end the strong contradiction is reversed: **Grandpa's memory is perfect and the adults forget things**.

# 🟢 PART B — I CANNOT REMEMBER MY MOTHER (POEM)

## 🎭 THEME

The poem is about **the tender memory and loss of the mother's presence**. The poet cannot recall his mother's face literally, since she passed away when he was young, yet he feels her **presence in every gentle sensation** — in the tune she hummed, in the scent of shiuli flowers and temple worship in autumn, and in the calm of the blue sky. The theme is the **undying mother–child bond**, carried through the senses.

---

## 💡 CENTRAL IDEA

**Even when the mother cannot be remembered, her presence is felt everywhere.** The song she used to hum, the autumn fragrance that touched her temple, and the vast blue sky — these are the mediums that make the mother alive in the poet's senses, so she is lost but never gone. **Love endures even when memory fades.**

---

## 📜 STANZA-WISE EXPLANATION (correct & complete)

### Stanza 1 — The Humming Lullaby

> I cannot remember my mother
> only sometimes in the midst of my play
> a tune seems to hover over my playthings,
> the tune of some song that she used to
> hum while rocking my cradle.

**Meaning:**
- The poet begins with the refrain *"I cannot remember my mother"* — he does not have any clear, literal memory of her face.
- However, **"only sometimes in the midst of my play"**, a **tune seems to hover** over his toys.
- That tune is the **song she used to hum** while rocking his cradle.
- **Sense used:** auditory (he hears the hum).
- **Emotion:** tender; the play becomes a doorway to her presence.

### Stanza 2 — The Fragrance of Shiuli and Temple

> I cannot remember my mother
> but when in the early autumn morning
> the smell of the shiuli flowers floats in the air
> the scent of the morning service in the temple
> comes to me as the scent of my mother.

**Meaning:**
- In the early **autumn** morning, the **smell of shiuli flowers** floats in the air.
- The **scent** of the morning service (the prayers, incense, marigolds) comes to him "as the scent of my mother."
- **Sense used:** olfactory — smell.
- The mother is associated with the fragrant, holy presence of the temple and the seasonal shiuli blooms.

### Stanza 3 — The Sky and the Mother's Gaze

> I cannot remember my mother
> only when from my bedroom window I send
> my eyes into the blue of the distant sky,
> I feel that the stillness of
> my mother's gaze on my face
> has spread all over the sky.

**Meaning:**
- Looking from the **window at the blue sky**, the poet feels the **"stillness of my mother's gaze"** — the calm, attentive eyes of the mother watching over him.
- That gaze has **"spread all over the sky"** — her love is **vast, serene and all-enveloping** like the sky.
- **Sense used:** visual — the blue, boundless sky; **symbolism**: the sky is the mother's endless, peaceful gaze.

## ❓ IMPORTANT QUESTIONS (Competency-based) — with answers

1. **What does the poet remember during play?** — The **tune of some song** that his mother used to hum; not her face.

2. **In Stanza 2, what reminds the poet of his mother?** — The **smell of shiuli flowers** and the **scent of the morning service in the temple** — in early autumn morning.

3. **'The stillness of my mother's gaze' — what does it suggest?** — The stillness is **the calm and peace of the mother's love**, which suffuses the entire sky.

4. **Setting of each stanza —** Stanza 1: play (outdoors / indoors). Stanza 2: autumn morning garden (outdoors). Stanza 3: bedroom window sky (outdoors). — (The sky suggests the **element of the mother's presence**, i.e., the vast sky as her symbol.)

5. **The refrain —** "I cannot remember my mother" is repeated in every stanza — **refrain**. It reinforces the **pervasive loss** and the **tender longing**; it also creates **musical rhythm**.

6. **What is the poet's attitude/tone?** — **tender, nostalgic, melancholic, and serene** — gentle sorrow, not harsh despair.

7. **Use of alliteration —** e.g. (i) "**s**mell of the **s**hiuli flowers" (the repeated 's'), (ii) "**s**tillness of my mother's gaze" (the repeated 's').

8. **Onomatopoeia —** "**hum**ming" (the word 'hum'imitates the soft singing sound).

9. **Imagery in the poem —** the tune hovering over the playthings (auditory), the shiuli fragrance (olfactory), and the blue sky with the mother's gaze (visual) — powerful, sense-alive images that evoke the mother without showing her face.

10. **Why does the poet call it "the scent of my mother"?** — The poet, having only fragments of memory of the mother, links her to the smell of the flowers and the temple — an odour-memory: the mother's scent is that of shiuli and the temple garlands.
